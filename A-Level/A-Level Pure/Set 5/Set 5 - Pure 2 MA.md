@marks 10
@question
(a) $y = \frac{1}{x^{x}} = x^{-x}$ \\
Take natural logs of both sides: \\
$\ln y = \ln(x^{-x})$ \\
Use the power law: \\
$\ln y = -x\ln x$ as required \\
(b) Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$: \\
$\frac{d}{dx}(\ln y) = \frac{1}{y}\frac{dy}{dx}$ \\
Product rule for $x\ln x$: $\frac{d}{dx}(x\ln x) = 1 \times \ln x + x \times \frac{1}{x} = \ln x + 1$ \\
$\frac{d}{dx}(-x\ln x) = -\ln x - 1$ \\
$\frac{1}{y}\frac{dy}{dx} = -\ln x - 1$ \\
Multiply both sides by $y$: \\
$\frac{dy}{dx} = y(-\ln x - 1)$ \\
Replace $y$ by $x^{-x}$ and factorise $-1$: \\
$\frac{dy}{dx} = -x^{-x}(\ln x + 1)$ as required \\
(c) At a stationary point, $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$: \\
$-x^{-x}(\ln x + 1) = 0$ \\
$x^{-x}$ is never zero, so \\
$\ln x + 1 = 0$ \\
$\ln x = -1$ \\
$x = e^{-1} = \frac{1}{e}$ \\
$y = x^{-x} = \left( e^{-1} \right)^{-e^{-1}}$ \\
$= e^{(-1) \times (-e^{-1})}$ \\
$= e^{e^{-1}}$ \\
The stationary point is $\left( \frac{1}{e}, e^{\frac{1}{e}} \right)$ \\
(d) For $x < \frac{1}{e}$: $\ln x + 1 < 0$, so $\frac{dy}{dx} > 0$ and the curve is rising \\
For $x > \frac{1}{e}$: $\ln x + 1 > 0$, so $\frac{dy}{dx} < 0$ and the curve is falling \\
So the stationary point is a maximum, with value $e^{\frac{1}{e}} = 1.44$ \\
As $x \to 0$, $g(x) \to 1$, and as $x \to \infty$, $g(x) \to 0$, but $g(x) > 0$ always \\

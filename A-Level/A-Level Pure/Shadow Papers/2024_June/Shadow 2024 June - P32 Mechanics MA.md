@topic Particle at rest, limiting friction
@marks 3
@question
(a) The plane is horizontal and $P$ is at rest, so the normal reaction balances the weight: \\
$R = 0.4g$ \\
$R = 0.4 \times 9.8$ \\
$R = 3.92$ N \\
(b) The particle is in limiting equilibrium, so friction is at its maximum: $F = \mu R$ \\
$F = \frac{3}{7} \times 3.92$ \\
$3.92 \div 7 = 0.56$ \\
$F = 3 \times 0.56$ \\
$F = 1.68$ N \\
The horizontal force $X$ just balances the friction: \\
$X = F$ \\
$X = 1.68$ (or 1.7 to 2 significant figures)

@q 2
@topic Speed-time graph, athlete model
@marks 8
@question
(a) Acceleration is the gradient of the speed-time graph \\
The speed goes from 0 to 8 m s$^{-1}$ in 4 s: \\
$a = \frac{8 - 0}{4}$ \\
$a = 2$ m s$^{-2}$ \\
(b) Distance is the area under the speed-time graph from $t = 0$ to $t = 50$ \\
The area is a triangle (0 to 4 s) plus a rectangle (4 to 50 s) \\
Triangle: $\frac{1}{2} \times 4 \times 8 = 16$ \\
Rectangle: the width is $50 - 4 = 46$, the height is 8: $46 \times 8 = 368$ \\
Total: $16 + 368 = 384$ \\
The distance covered in the first 50 s is 384 m \\
(c) The race is 400 m, so the distance covered from $t = 50$ to $t = 53$ is: \\
$400 - 384 = 16$ m \\
This time interval is 3 s long, with the speed falling from 8 to $U$ \\
The area is a trapezium with parallel sides 8 and $U$ and width 3: \\
$\frac{1}{2} \times 3 \times (8 + U) = 16$ \\
Multiply both sides by 2: \\
$3(8 + U) = 32$ \\
Expand the bracket: \\
$24 + 3U = 32$ \\
Subtract 24: \\
$3U = 8$ \\
Divide by 3: \\
$U = \frac{8}{3}$ \\
$U = 2\frac{2}{3}$ m s$^{-1}$

@q 3
@topic Particle sliding down rough slope
@marks 7
@question
(a) $\tan \alpha = \frac{7}{24}$, so a right angled triangle has opposite 7, adjacent 24 and hypotenuse $\sqrt{49 + 576} = 25$ \\
$\sin \alpha = \frac{7}{25}$ and $\cos \alpha = \frac{24}{25}$ \\
There is no motion perpendicular to the plane, so resolve perpendicular to the plane: \\
$R = mg\cos \alpha$ \\
$R = mg \times \frac{24}{25}$ \\
$R = \frac{24}{25}mg$ \\
(b) $P$ slides down the plane, so friction acts up the plane and equals $\mu R$: \\
$F = \mu R$ \\
$F = \frac{24}{25}\mu mg$ \\
The weight component down the plane is $mg\sin \alpha = \frac{7}{25}mg$ \\
Equation of motion down the plane ($F = ma$): \\
$mg\sin \alpha - F = ma$ \\
$\frac{7}{25}mg - \frac{24}{25}\mu mg = ma$ \\
Divide every term by $m$: \\
$a = \frac{7}{25}g - \frac{24}{25}\mu g$ \\
Take out the common factor $\frac{1}{25}g$: \\
$a = \frac{1}{25}g(7 - 24\mu)$ as required \\
(c) If $\mu = \frac{3}{8}$ then $24\mu = 24 \times \frac{3}{8} = 9$ \\
The formula would give $a = \frac{1}{25}g(7 - 9)$, which is negative, so $P$ cannot slide down \\
More simply, $\tan \alpha = \frac{7}{24} = 0.292$ and $\mu = \frac{3}{8} = 0.375$, so $\mu > \tan \alpha$ \\
Friction can hold the particle, so $P$ would not move

@q 4
@topic Position vector, bearing, velocity
@marks 11
@question
(a) Put $t = 3$ into $\mathbf{r} = ct^{2}\mathbf{i} - 4t^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{j}$ \\
$ct^{2} = 9c$ \\
$4t^{\frac{1}{2}} = 4\sqrt{3}$ \\
$\mathbf{r} = 9c\mathbf{i} - 4\sqrt{3}\mathbf{j}$ \\
The bearing is measured clockwise from north \\
A bearing of 150 degrees is in the south east quarter, 30 degrees from due south \\
So $P$ is $9c$ east of $O$ and $4\sqrt{3}$ south of $O$ \\
The angle between the line $OP$ and the south direction is $180 - 150 = 30$ degrees \\
In the right angled triangle, the east distance is opposite this angle and the south distance is adjacent: \\
$\tan 30 = \frac{9c}{4\sqrt{3}}$ \\
$\tan 30 = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ \\
$\frac{9c}{4\sqrt{3}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ \\
Multiply both sides by $4\sqrt{3}$: \\
$9c = 4$ \\
Divide by 9: \\
$c = \frac{4}{9}$ as required \\
(b) Velocity is the derivative of position: \\
$\mathbf{r} = \frac{4}{9}t^{2}\mathbf{i} - 4t^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{j}$ \\
$\frac{4}{9}t^{2} \to \frac{8}{9}t$ \\
$-4t^{\frac{1}{2}} \to -4 \times \frac{1}{2}t^{-\frac{1}{2}} = -2t^{-\frac{1}{2}}$ \\
$\mathbf{v} = \frac{8}{9}t\mathbf{i} - 2t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{j}$ \\
Put $t = 3$: \\
$\frac{8}{9} \times 3 = \frac{8}{3}$ \\
$2 \times 3^{-\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{2}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}$ \\
$\mathbf{v} = \frac{8}{3}\mathbf{i} - \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}\mathbf{j}$ \\
Speed $= \sqrt{\left( \frac{8}{3} \right)^{2} + \left( \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3} \right)^{2}}$ \\
$\left( \frac{8}{3} \right)^{2} = \frac{64}{9}$ \\
$\left( \frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3} \right)^{2} = \frac{4 \times 3}{9} = \frac{12}{9}$ \\
Speed $= \sqrt{\frac{64 + 12}{9}}$ \\
$= \sqrt{\frac{76}{9}}$ \\
$= \frac{\sqrt{76}}{3}$ \\
$\sqrt{76} = \sqrt{4 \times 19} = 2\sqrt{19}$ \\
Speed $= \frac{2\sqrt{19}}{3}$ \\
Speed $= 2.91$ m s$^{-1}$ \\
(c) Acceleration is the derivative of velocity: \\
$\mathbf{v} = \frac{8}{9}t\mathbf{i} - 2t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{j}$ \\
$\frac{8}{9}t \to \frac{8}{9}$ \\
$-2t^{-\frac{1}{2}} \to -2 \times \left( -\frac{1}{2} \right)t^{-\frac{3}{2}} = t^{-\frac{3}{2}}$ \\
$\mathbf{a} = \frac{8}{9}\mathbf{i} + t^{-\frac{3}{2}}\mathbf{j}$ \\
At $t = T$ the acceleration is in the direction of $64\mathbf{i} + 9\mathbf{j}$, so the components are in the ratio $64 : 9$ \\
$\frac{\mathbf{i} \text{ component}}{\mathbf{j} \text{ component}} = \frac{64}{9}$ \\
$\frac{\frac{8}{9}}{T^{-\frac{3}{2}}} = \frac{64}{9}$ \\
Multiply both sides by $T^{-\frac{3}{2}}$: \\
$\frac{8}{9} = \frac{64}{9}T^{-\frac{3}{2}}$ \\
Multiply by $\frac{9}{64}$: \\
$T^{-\frac{3}{2}} = \frac{8}{64}$ \\
$T^{-\frac{3}{2}} = \frac{1}{8}$ \\
Take the reciprocal: \\
$T^{\frac{3}{2}} = 8$ \\
Raise both sides to the power $\frac{2}{3}$: \\
$T = 8^{\frac{2}{3}}$ \\
$8^{\frac{1}{3}} = 2$, so $8^{\frac{2}{3}} = 4$ \\
$T = 4$

@q 5
@topic Projectile path equation, height
@marks 12
@question
(a) Take $g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$ \\
$\tan \alpha = \frac{5}{12}$, so a right angled triangle has opposite 5, adjacent 12 and hypotenuse 13 \\
$\sin \alpha = \frac{5}{13}$ and $\cos \alpha = \frac{12}{13}$ \\
Horizontal motion has constant speed $91\cos \alpha$: \\
$x = 91\cos \alpha \times t$ \\
Make $t$ the subject: \\
$t = \frac{x}{91\cos \alpha}$ \\
Vertical motion: use $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^{2}$ with $u = 91\sin \alpha$ and $a = -g$: \\
$y = 91\sin \alpha \times t - \frac{1}{2}gt^{2}$ \\
Substitute for $t$: \\
$y = 91\sin \alpha \times \frac{x}{91\cos \alpha} - \frac{1}{2}g\left( \frac{x}{91\cos \alpha} \right)^{2}$ \\
Simplify the first term: the 91s cancel, and $\frac{\sin \alpha}{\cos \alpha} = \tan \alpha = \frac{5}{12}$: \\
$91\sin \alpha \times \frac{x}{91\cos \alpha} = \frac{5}{12}x$ \\
Simplify the second term: $91\cos \alpha = 91 \times \frac{12}{13} = 84$ \\
$\left( \frac{x}{84} \right)^{2} = \frac{x^{2}}{7056}$ \\
$\frac{1}{2}g = 4.9$ \\
$4.9 \times \frac{x^{2}}{7056} = \frac{4.9x^{2}}{7056}$ \\
$\frac{4.9}{7056} = \frac{1}{1440}$ \\
$y = \frac{5}{12}x - \frac{1}{1440}x^{2}$ as required \\
(b) $O$ and $A$ are both on the ground, so $y = 0$ at $A$ \\
$0 = \frac{5}{12}x - \frac{1}{1440}x^{2}$ \\
Factorise out $x$: \\
$0 = x\left( \frac{5}{12} - \frac{x}{1440} \right)$ \\
$x = 0$ is the point $O$ \\
The other solution: $\frac{5}{12} = \frac{x}{1440}$ \\
Multiply by 1440: \\
$x = \frac{5 \times 1440}{12}$ \\
$x = 600$ \\
$OA = 600$ m \\
(c) The greatest height is half way along, at $x = \frac{600}{2} = 300$ \\
$H = \frac{5}{12} \times 300 - \frac{1}{1440} \times 300^{2}$ \\
$\frac{5}{12} \times 300 = 125$ \\
$300^{2} = 90000$ \\
$\frac{90000}{1440} = 62.5$ \\
$H = 125 - 62.5$ \\
$H = 62.5$ m \\
(Check: $H = \frac{(91\sin \alpha)^{2}}{2g} = \frac{35^{2}}{19.6} = \frac{1225}{19.6} = 62.5$) \\
(d) Air resistance slows the stone down, so it does not rise as high \\
So $H$ is greater than $K$ \\
(e) One limitation: the value $g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$ is not exactly accurate \\
(Or the stone may spin, or be affected by the wind)

@q 6
@topic Rod resting on a smooth peg
@marks 9
@question
(a) $\tan \theta = \frac{3}{4}$, so a right angled triangle has opposite 3, adjacent 4 and hypotenuse 5 \\
$\sin \theta = \frac{3}{5}$ and $\cos \theta = \frac{4}{5}$ \\
The rod is uniform, so its weight $Mg$ acts at the midpoint $G$, which is $2a$ from $A$ \\
The peg is smooth, so the force $S$ at $C$ is perpendicular to the rod \\
$C$ is $2.5a$ from $A$ \\
Take moments about $A$ (the forces at $A$ have no moment) \\
Moment of $S$: $S \times 2.5a$ \\
Moment of the weight: the horizontal distance from $A$ to $G$ is $2a\cos \theta$, so the moment is $Mg \times 2a\cos \theta$ \\
$S \times 2.5a = Mg \times 2a\cos \theta$ \\
Put in $\cos \theta = \frac{4}{5}$: \\
$2.5aS = 2aMg \times \frac{4}{5}$ \\
$2.5aS = 1.6aMg$ \\
Divide both sides by $2.5a$: \\
$S = \frac{1.6}{2.5}Mg$ \\
$S = 0.64Mg$ \\
$S = \frac{16}{25}Mg$ as required \\
(b) At $A$ there is a vertical reaction $R$ and a horizontal friction force $F$ \\
The force $S$ is perpendicular to the rod, so it makes angle $\theta$ with the vertical: \\
Vertical component of $S$: $S\cos \theta$ (upwards) \\
Horizontal component of $S$: $S\sin \theta$ (towards the left, away from the direction the rod leans) \\
Resolve horizontally: \\
$F = S\sin \theta$ \\
$F = \frac{16}{25}Mg \times \frac{3}{5}$ \\
$F = \frac{48}{125}Mg$ \\
Resolve vertically: \\
$R + S\cos \theta = Mg$ \\
$R = Mg - S\cos \theta$ \\
$S\cos \theta = \frac{16}{25}Mg \times \frac{4}{5}$ \\
$= \frac{64}{125}Mg$ \\
$R = Mg - \frac{64}{125}Mg$ \\
Write $Mg$ over 125: $Mg = \frac{125}{125}Mg$ \\
$R = \frac{125 - 64}{125}Mg$ \\
$R = \frac{61}{125}Mg$ \\
The rod is in limiting equilibrium, so $F = \mu R$: \\
$\frac{48}{125}Mg = \mu \times \frac{61}{125}Mg$ \\
Multiply both sides by $\frac{125}{Mg}$: \\
$48 = 61\mu$ \\
Divide by 61: \\
$\mu = \frac{48}{61}$ \\
$\mu = 0.7869$ \\
$\mu = 0.787$ to 3 significant figures

@q 1
@topic Velocity to acceleration and position
@marks 6
@question
(a) Acceleration is the derivative of velocity: $\mathbf{a} = \frac{d\mathbf{v}}{dt}$ \\
$\mathbf{v} = 8t\mathbf{i} - 15t^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathbf{j}$ \\
Differentiate each component: \\
$8t \to 8$ \\
$-15t^{\frac{3}{2}} \to -15 \times \frac{3}{2}t^{\frac{1}{2}} = -\frac{45}{2}t^{\frac{1}{2}}$ \\
$\mathbf{a} = 8\mathbf{i} - \frac{45}{2}t^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{j}$ \\
Put $t = 9$: $t^{\frac{1}{2}} = 3$ \\
$\frac{45}{2} \times 3 = \frac{135}{2}$ \\
$\mathbf{a} = 8\mathbf{i} - \frac{135}{2}\mathbf{j}$ m s$^{-2}$ \\
(b) Position is the integral of velocity: $\mathbf{r} = \int \mathbf{v} \, dt$ \\
Integrate each component (add 1 to the power, divide by the new power): \\
$\int 8t \, dt = 4t^{2}$ \\
$\int -15t^{\frac{3}{2}} \, dt = -15 \times \frac{t^{\frac{5}{2}}}{\frac{5}{2}} = -6t^{\frac{5}{2}}$ \\
$\mathbf{r} = 4t^{2}\mathbf{i} - 6t^{\frac{5}{2}}\mathbf{j} + \mathbf{c}$ \\
When $t = 0$, $\mathbf{r} = -5\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j}$, so $\mathbf{c} = -5\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j}$ \\
$\mathbf{r} = (4t^{2} - 5)\mathbf{i} + (3 - 6t^{\frac{5}{2}})\mathbf{j}$ \\
Put $t = 4$: \\
$4t^{2} = 4 \times 16 = 64$ \\
$t^{\frac{5}{2}} = (\sqrt{4})^{5} = 2^{5} = 32$ \\
$6t^{\frac{5}{2}} = 6 \times 32 = 192$ \\
$\mathbf{i}$ component: $64 - 5 = 59$ \\
$\mathbf{j}$ component: $3 - 192 = -189$ \\
$\mathbf{r} = 59\mathbf{i} - 189\mathbf{j}$ m

@q 2
@topic Constant acceleration vectors, direction
@marks 6
@question
(a) Use $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{a}t$ with $\mathbf{u} = -7\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j}$ and $\mathbf{a} = 3\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j}$ \\
$\mathbf{v} = (-7\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j}) + (3\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j})t$ \\
$\mathbf{i}$ component: $-7 + 3t$ \\
$\mathbf{j}$ component: $1 + 4t$ \\
$\mathbf{v} = (-7 + 3t)\mathbf{i} + (1 + 4t)\mathbf{j}$ \\
At $t = T$ the velocity is in the direction of $2\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j}$, so the components are in the ratio $2 : 3$ \\
$\frac{\mathbf{j} \text{ component}}{\mathbf{i} \text{ component}} = \frac{3}{2}$ \\
$\frac{1 + 4T}{-7 + 3T} = \frac{3}{2}$ \\
Cross multiply: \\
$2(1 + 4T) = 3(-7 + 3T)$ \\
Expand both brackets: \\
$2 + 8T = -21 + 9T$ \\
Subtract $8T$ from both sides: \\
$2 = -21 + T$ \\
Add 21: \\
$T = 23$ \\
(Check: at $t = 23$, $\mathbf{v} = 62\mathbf{i} + 93\mathbf{j} = 31(2\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j})$) \\
(b) Use $\mathbf{s} = \mathbf{u}t + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}t^{2}$ with $t = 4$ \\
$\mathbf{u}t = 4(-7\mathbf{i} + \mathbf{j}) = -28\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j}$ \\
$\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}t^{2} = \frac{1}{2}(3\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j}) \times 16$ \\
$= 8(3\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j})$ \\
$= 24\mathbf{i} + 32\mathbf{j}$ \\
$\mathbf{s} = (-28 + 24)\mathbf{i} + (4 + 32)\mathbf{j}$ \\
$\mathbf{s} = -4\mathbf{i} + 36\mathbf{j}$ \\
$AB = |\mathbf{s}|$ \\
$AB = \sqrt{(-4)^{2} + 36^{2}}$ \\
$= \sqrt{16 + 1296}$ \\
$= \sqrt{1312}$ \\
$AB = 36.2$ m

@q 3
@topic Connected blocks on rough slope
@marks 12
@question
(a) $\tan \alpha = \frac{5}{12}$, so a right angled triangle has opposite 5, adjacent 12 and hypotenuse $\sqrt{25 + 144} = 13$ \\
$\sin \alpha = \frac{5}{13}$ and $\cos \alpha = \frac{12}{13}$ \\
Block $A$: there is no motion perpendicular to the plane, so resolve perpendicular to the plane \\
$R = 2mg\cos \alpha$ \\
$R = 2mg \times \frac{12}{13}$ \\
$R = \frac{24mg}{13}$ \\
$A$ is moving up the slope, so friction acts down the slope and is at its limiting value: $F = \mu R$ \\
$F = \frac{1}{3} \times \frac{24mg}{13}$ \\
$F = \frac{8mg}{13}$ \\
The component of $A$'s weight down the slope: \\
$2mg\sin \alpha = 2mg \times \frac{5}{13}$ \\
$= \frac{10mg}{13}$ \\
Let the common acceleration be $a$ ($A$ up the slope, $B$ downwards) \\
Equation of motion for $A$ (up the slope is positive): \\
$T - F - 2mg\sin \alpha = 2ma$ \\
$T - \frac{8mg}{13} - \frac{10mg}{13} = 2ma$ \\
Collect the weights: $\frac{8mg}{13} + \frac{10mg}{13} = \frac{18mg}{13}$ \\
Equation 1: $T - \frac{18mg}{13} = 2ma$ \\
Equation of motion for $B$ (downwards is positive): \\
Equation 2: $3mg - T = 3ma$ \\
Make $ma$ the subject of each equation: \\
From equation 1: $ma = \frac{1}{2}\left( T - \frac{18mg}{13} \right)$ \\
From equation 2: $ma = \frac{1}{3}(3mg - T)$ \\
Set them equal: \\
$\frac{1}{2}\left( T - \frac{18mg}{13} \right) = \frac{1}{3}(3mg - T)$ \\
Multiply both sides by 6: \\
$3\left( T - \frac{18mg}{13} \right) = 2(3mg - T)$ \\
Expand both brackets: \\
$3T - \frac{54mg}{13} = 6mg - 2T$ \\
Add $2T$ and add $\frac{54mg}{13}$ to both sides: \\
$5T = 6mg + \frac{54mg}{13}$ \\
Write $6mg$ over 13: $6mg = \frac{78mg}{13}$ \\
$5T = \frac{78mg + 54mg}{13}$ \\
$5T = \frac{132mg}{13}$ \\
Divide by 5: \\
$T = \frac{132}{65}mg$ as required \\
(b) When $A$ comes to rest, it is no longer sliding, and the string is slack because $B$ is on the ground \\
The maximum friction is $F_{\text{max}} = \frac{8mg}{13}$ \\
The force trying to pull $A$ down the slope is its weight component: $2mg\sin \alpha = \frac{10mg}{13}$ \\
Compare them: $\frac{8mg}{13} < \frac{10mg}{13}$ \\
Friction cannot be large enough to hold $A$ \\
So $A$ will not remain at rest: it will slide back down the plane \\
(c) Refinement 1: include air resistance in the model \\
Refinement 2: model the string as extensible (or model the pulley as rough, or the blocks as having size)

@q 4
@topic Ramp resting on a drum, moments
@marks 11
@question
(a) The drum is smooth, so there is no friction between the drum and the ramp \\
With no friction, there is no force along the ramp from the drum \\
So the whole reaction is perpendicular to the ramp \\
(b) $\tan \theta = \frac{5}{12}$, so $\sin \theta = \frac{5}{13}$ and $\cos \theta = \frac{12}{13}$ \\
Forces on the ramp: \\
Weight $20g$ acts at the midpoint of the ramp, which is 4 m from $A$ \\
The reaction $N$ at $C$ is perpendicular to the ramp, 5 m from $A$ \\
At $A$ there is a vertical force $R$ (up) from the ground, and a horizontal friction force $F$ \\
Take moments about $A$, so $R$ and $F$ have no moment: \\
Moment of $N$ about $A$: $N \times 5$ \\
Moment of the weight about $A$: the horizontal distance from $A$ to the midpoint is $4\cos \theta$, so $20g \times 4\cos \theta$ \\
$5N = 20g \times 4\cos \theta$ \\
$5N = 80g\cos \theta$ \\
Divide by 5: \\
$N = 16g\cos \theta$ \\
$N = 16 \times 9.8 \times \frac{12}{13}$ \\
$16 \times 9.8 = 156.8$ \\
$156.8 \times \frac{12}{13} = 144.74$ \\
$N = 144.74$ N \\
The direction of $N$ makes an angle $\theta$ with the vertical: it has an upward component $N\cos \theta$ and a horizontal component $N\sin \theta$ pointing away from the drum (back towards $A$) \\
Resolve vertically: \\
$R + N\cos \theta = 20g$ \\
$R + 144.74 \times \frac{12}{13} = 20 \times 9.8$ \\
$144.74 \times \frac{12}{13} = 133.60$ \\
$R + 133.60 = 196$ \\
Subtract 133.60: \\
$R = 62.40$ N \\
Resolve horizontally: \\
$F = N\sin \theta$ \\
$F = 144.74 \times \frac{5}{13}$ \\
$F = 55.67$ N \\
The resultant force at $A$ is made up of $R$ and $F$ at right angles to each other \\
Use Pythagoras: \\
$|\text{Force}| = \sqrt{R^{2} + F^{2}}$ \\
$R^{2} = 62.40^{2} = 3893.8$ \\
$F^{2} = 55.67^{2} = 3099.1$ \\
$|\text{Force}| = \sqrt{3893.8 + 3099.1}$ \\
$= \sqrt{6992.9}$ \\
$= 83.6$ N \\
(c) If the centre of mass is closer to $A$, the weight acts nearer to $A$ and has a smaller moment about $A$ \\
Moments about $A$: $5N = 20g \times (\text{distance of the centre of mass from } A) \times \cos \theta$ \\
A smaller distance gives a smaller $N$ \\
So the normal reaction at $C$ will decrease

@q 5
@topic Two projectiles collide
@marks 13
@question
(a) Take $g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$ \\
Resolve the initial velocity of $P$ (20 m s$^{-1}$ at 30 degrees): \\
Horizontal: $20\cos 30 = 20 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ \\
$= 10\sqrt{3}$ \\
$= 17.32$ m s$^{-1}$ \\
Vertical: $20\sin 30 = 20 \times \frac{1}{2} = 10$ m s$^{-1}$ \\
There is no horizontal acceleration, so the horizontal speed stays at $10\sqrt{3}$ \\
For the vertical speed use $v = u + at$ with $u = 10$, $a = -9.8$, $t = 1$: \\
$v = 10 - 9.8 \times 1$ \\
$v = 0.2$ m s$^{-1}$ upwards \\
Speed $= \sqrt{\left( 10\sqrt{3} \right)^{2} + 0.2^{2}}$ \\
$\left( 10\sqrt{3} \right)^{2} = 300$ \\
$0.2^{2} = 0.04$ \\
Speed $= \sqrt{300.04}$ \\
Speed $= 17.3$ m s$^{-1}$ \\
Direction: the angle above the horizontal is $\phi$ where \\
$\tan \phi = \frac{0.2}{10\sqrt{3}}$ \\
$\tan \phi = 0.011547$ \\
$\phi = 0.662$ degrees \\
$P$ is moving at 17.3 m s$^{-1}$ at 0.662 degrees above the horizontal (moving towards $B$) \\
(b) At the collision the balls are at the same point \\
Horizontal: the two balls have together covered the 50 m between $A$ and $B$ in 1 s \\
$P$ has gone $20\cos 30 \times 1 = 17.32$ m from $A$ \\
$Q$ has gone $u\cos \theta \times 1$ from $B$ towards $A$ \\
$17.32 + u\cos \theta = 50$ \\
Subtract 17.32: \\
Equation 1: $u\cos \theta = 50 - 20\cos 30$ \\
$u\cos \theta = 32.68$ \\
Vertical: both are at the same height at $t = 1$ \\
Height of $P$: $20\sin 30 \times 1 - \frac{1}{2}g \times 1^{2}$ \\
Height of $Q$: $u\sin \theta \times 1 - \frac{1}{2}g \times 1^{2}$ \\
Set equal: \\
$20\sin 30 - \frac{g}{2} = u\sin \theta - \frac{g}{2}$ \\
The $\frac{g}{2}$ terms cancel: \\
Equation 2: $u\sin \theta = 20\sin 30$ \\
$u\sin \theta = 10$ \\
Divide equation 2 by equation 1: \\
$\frac{u\sin \theta}{u\cos \theta} = \frac{10}{32.68}$ \\
$\tan \theta = 0.30601$ \\
$\theta = 17.01$ degrees \\
(i) $\theta = 17.0$ degrees \\
Use equation 2 to find $u$: \\
$u = \frac{10}{\sin 17.014}$ \\
$\sin 17.014 = 0.29261$ \\
$u = 34.18$ \\
(ii) $u = 34.2$ m s$^{-1}$ \\
(Check with equation 1: $34.18 \times \cos 17.014 = 34.18 \times 0.95623 = 32.68$) \\
(c) One limitation: the balls are treated as particles, but real balls have a size, and may spin \\
(Wind could also change their paths)

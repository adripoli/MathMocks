@q 1
@topic Constant acceleration, toy vectors
@marks 4
@question
(a) Use $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{a}t$ with $\mathbf{u} = 6\mathbf{j}$, $\mathbf{a} = 3\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j}$ and $t = 4$ \\
$\mathbf{a}t = 4(3\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j}) = 12\mathbf{i} - 8\mathbf{j}$ \\
$\mathbf{v} = 6\mathbf{j} + 12\mathbf{i} - 8\mathbf{j}$ \\
$\mathbf{i}$ component: 12 \\
$\mathbf{j}$ component: $6 - 8 = -2$ \\
$\mathbf{v} = 12\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j}$ m s$^{-1}$ \\
(b) Use $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{r}_{0} + \mathbf{u}t + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}t^{2}$ with $\mathbf{r}_{0} = 2\mathbf{i} - 8\mathbf{j}$ and $t = 5$ \\
$\mathbf{u}t = 5 \times 6\mathbf{j} = 30\mathbf{j}$ \\
$\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}t^{2} = \frac{1}{2}(3\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j}) \times 25$ \\
$= 12.5(3\mathbf{i} - 2\mathbf{j})$ \\
$= 37.5\mathbf{i} - 25\mathbf{j}$ \\
$\mathbf{r} = (2\mathbf{i} - 8\mathbf{j}) + 30\mathbf{j} + (37.5\mathbf{i} - 25\mathbf{j})$ \\
$\mathbf{i}$ component: $2 + 37.5 = 39.5$ \\
$\mathbf{j}$ component: $-8 + 30 - 25 = -3$ \\
$\mathbf{r} = 39.5\mathbf{i} - 3\mathbf{j}$ m

@q 2
@topic Connected particles, rough slope, pulley
@marks 12
@question
(a) $\tan \alpha = \frac{5}{12}$, so $\sin \alpha = \frac{5}{13}$ and $\cos \alpha = \frac{12}{13}$ \\
Stone $P$ (mass $8m$) moves down the slope, since its weight component is the larger of the two pulls \\
Forces on $P$ along the slope: weight component $8mg\sin \alpha$ down the slope, friction $F$ up the slope, tension $T$ up the slope \\
Equation of motion for $P$ (down the slope is positive): \\
$8mg\sin \alpha - F - T = 8ma$ \\
(b) Resolve perpendicular to the plane for $P$: \\
$R = 8mg\cos \alpha$ \\
$R = 8mg \times \frac{12}{13}$ \\
$R = \frac{96mg}{13}$ \\
Friction: $F = \frac{1}{8}R$ \\
$F = \frac{1}{8} \times \frac{96mg}{13}$ \\
$F = \frac{12mg}{13}$ \\
The weight component: $8mg\sin \alpha = 8mg \times \frac{5}{13}$ \\
$= \frac{40mg}{13}$ \\
Put into the equation of motion for $P$: \\
Equation 1: $\frac{40mg}{13} - \frac{12mg}{13} - T = 8ma$ \\
$\frac{28mg}{13} - T = 8ma$ \\
Stone $Q$ (mass $2m$) moves up with the same acceleration $a$ \\
Equation of motion for $Q$ (up is positive): \\
Equation 2: $T - 2mg = 2ma$ \\
Add equation 1 and equation 2 to remove $T$: \\
$\frac{28mg}{13} - T + T - 2mg = 8ma + 2ma$ \\
$\frac{28mg}{13} - 2mg = 10ma$ \\
Write $2mg$ over 13: $2mg = \frac{26mg}{13}$ \\
$\frac{28mg - 26mg}{13} = 10ma$ \\
$\frac{2mg}{13} = 10ma$ \\
Divide by $10m$: \\
$a = \frac{2g}{130}$ \\
$a = \frac{g}{65}$ as required \\
(c) $P$ starts from rest, so the graph starts at the origin \\
The acceleration is constant, $\frac{g}{65}$, so the speed increases steadily \\
The velocity-time graph is a straight line through the origin with a positive gradient \\
(Diagram: a straight line from the origin sloping upwards, ending where $Q$ is just about to reach the pulley) \\
The reason: the acceleration found in part (b) is constant, and the gradient of a velocity-time graph is the acceleration \\
(d) One limitation: the pulley may not be perfectly smooth (it would have some friction) \\
(Or the string may have mass, or the stones may not be particles)

@q 3
@topic Beam against smooth wall, friction
@marks 10
@question
(a) Forces on the beam: weight $2mg$ at the midpoint, which is $3a$ from $X$ \\
At $X$: a normal reaction $R$ upwards and a friction force $F$ along the ground, directed away from the wall \\
At $Y$: the wall is smooth, so the force $S$ is horizontal, pushing away from the wall \\
Take moments about $X$ \\
Moment of the weight: $2mg \times 3a\cos \theta = 6mga\cos \theta$ \\
(the horizontal distance from $X$ to the weight is $3a\cos \theta$) \\
Moment of $S$: the force $S$ is horizontal at $Y$, which is a height $6a\sin \theta$ above $X$ \\
So the moment is $S \times 6a\sin \theta$ \\
$6a S\sin \theta = 6mga\cos \theta$ \\
Divide by $6a\sin \theta$: \\
$S = mg\frac{\cos \theta}{\sin \theta}$ \\
$S = mg\cot \theta$ \\
Resolve horizontally: \\
$F = S$ \\
$F = mg\cot \theta$ \\
Resolve vertically: \\
$R = 2mg$ \\
The beam is in equilibrium, so friction is at most the limiting value: $F \leq \mu R$ \\
$mg\cot \theta \leq \mu \times 2mg$ \\
Divide by $2mg$: \\
$\frac{1}{2}\cot \theta \leq \mu$ \\
$\mu \geq \frac{1}{2}\cot \theta$ as required \\
(b) $\tan \theta = 3$, so $\cot \theta = \frac{1}{3}$ \\
A horizontal force $kmg$ is now applied at $X$ towards the wall \\
The beam is in limiting equilibrium, with the friction acting away from the wall (opposing the force $kmg$) \\
Take moments about $X$ again (the force $kmg$ and the forces at $X$ have no moment about $X$): \\
Let the wall force be $N$ \\
$6a N\sin \theta = 6mga\cos \theta$ \\
$N = mg\cot \theta$ \\
$N = \frac{mg}{3}$ \\
Resolve vertically: $R = 2mg$ \\
Limiting friction: $F = \mu R$ \\
$F = \frac{1}{5} \times 2mg$ \\
$F = \frac{2mg}{5}$ \\
Resolve horizontally: the force $kmg$ is towards the wall; the friction $F$ and the wall force $N$ both act away from the wall \\
$kmg = F + N$ \\
$kmg = \frac{2mg}{5} + \frac{mg}{3}$ \\
Divide by $mg$: \\
$k = \frac{2}{5} + \frac{1}{3}$ \\
$k = \frac{6}{15} + \frac{5}{15}$ \\
$k = \frac{11}{15}$ \\
$k = 0.733$ \\
$k = 0.73$ to 2 significant figures

@q 4
@topic Projectile from a cliff, speed at ground
@marks 10
@question
(a) $g = 10$ \\
$\tan \alpha = \frac{3}{4}$, so a right angled triangle has opposite 3, adjacent 4 and hypotenuse 5 \\
$\sin \alpha = \frac{3}{5}$ and $\cos \alpha = \frac{4}{5}$ \\
Initial vertical speed: $30\sin \alpha = 30 \times \frac{3}{5} = 18$ m s$^{-1}$ \\
Initial horizontal speed: $30\cos \alpha = 30 \times \frac{4}{5} = 24$ m s$^{-1}$ \\
Take up as positive. The object lands 35 m below $O$, so the vertical displacement is $-35$ \\
Use $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^{2}$ vertically: \\
$-35 = 18t - \frac{1}{2}(10)t^{2}$ \\
$-35 = 18t - 5t^{2}$ \\
Add $5t^{2}$ and subtract $18t$ from both sides: \\
$5t^{2} - 18t - 35 = 0$ \\
Factorise: \\
$(5t + 7)(t - 5) = 0$ \\
$t = -\frac{7}{5}$ or $t = 5$ \\
Time cannot be negative, so reject $t = -\frac{7}{5}$ \\
$t = 5$ seconds \\
(b) Horizontal velocity at $A$ stays the same: $24$ m s$^{-1}$ \\
Vertical velocity at $A$: $v = u + at$ \\
$v = 18 - 10 \times 5$ \\
$v = 18 - 50$ \\
$v = -32$ m s$^{-1}$ (downwards) \\
(Check with $v^{2} = u^{2} + 2as$: $v^{2} = 18^{2} + 2(10)(35) = 324 + 700 = 1024$, so $|v| = 32$) \\
Speed $= \sqrt{24^{2} + (-32)^{2}}$ \\
$24^{2} = 576$ \\
$32^{2} = 1024$ \\
Speed $= \sqrt{576 + 1024}$ \\
$= \sqrt{1600}$ \\
$= 40$ m s$^{-1}$ \\
(c) Modelling the object as a particle ignores its size and shape: for example, it could spin, or the wind could push it differently because of its shape

@q 5
@topic Velocity vector, direction, speed
@marks 14
@question
(a) Acceleration is the derivative of velocity: $\mathbf{a} = \frac{d\mathbf{v}}{dt}$ \\
$\mathbf{v} = 4t\mathbf{i} + 6t^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{j}$ \\
$4t \to 4$ \\
$6t^{\frac{1}{2}} \to 6 \times \frac{1}{2}t^{-\frac{1}{2}} = 3t^{-\frac{1}{2}}$ \\
$\mathbf{a} = 4\mathbf{i} + 3t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{j}$ m s$^{-2}$ \\
(b) $P$ is moving in the direction of $\mathbf{i} + 2\mathbf{j}$, so the $\mathbf{j}$ component of the velocity is twice the $\mathbf{i}$ component \\
$6t^{\frac{1}{2}} = 2 \times 4t$ \\
$6t^{\frac{1}{2}} = 8t$ \\
Divide both sides by $2t^{\frac{1}{2}}$ (allowed as $t > 0$): \\
$3 = 4t^{\frac{1}{2}}$ \\
Divide by 4: \\
$t^{\frac{1}{2}} = \frac{3}{4}$ \\
Square both sides: \\
$t = \frac{9}{16}$ \\
(c) Position is the integral of velocity: \\
$\int 4t \, dt = 2t^{2}$ \\
$\int 6t^{\frac{1}{2}} \, dt = 6 \times \frac{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\frac{3}{2}} = 4t^{\frac{3}{2}}$ \\
$\mathbf{r} = 2t^{2}\mathbf{i} + 4t^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathbf{j} + \mathbf{C}$ \\
At $t = 9$, $\mathbf{r} = 162\mathbf{i}$: \\
$2(9)^{2} = 2 \times 81 = 162$ \\
$9^{\frac{3}{2}} = (\sqrt{9})^{3} = 27$ \\
$4 \times 27 = 108$ \\
$162\mathbf{i} = 162\mathbf{i} + 108\mathbf{j} + \mathbf{C}$ \\
Subtract $162\mathbf{i} + 108\mathbf{j}$ from both sides: \\
$\mathbf{C} = -108\mathbf{j}$ \\
$\mathbf{r} = 2t^{2}\mathbf{i} + \left( 4t^{\frac{3}{2}} - 108 \right)\mathbf{j}$ \\
(d) The speed is the magnitude of the velocity \\
$\text{speed} = \sqrt{(4t)^{2} + \left( 6t^{\frac{1}{2}} \right)^{2}}$ \\
Set the speed to 20: \\
$(4t)^{2} + \left( 6t^{\frac{1}{2}} \right)^{2} = 20^{2}$ \\
$(4t)^{2} = 16t^{2}$ \\
$\left( 6t^{\frac{1}{2}} \right)^{2} = 36t$ \\
$16t^{2} + 36t = 400$ \\
Subtract 400 from both sides: \\
$16t^{2} + 36t - 400 = 0$ \\
Divide by 4: \\
$4t^{2} + 9t - 100 = 0$ \\
Factorise: \\
$(4t + 25)(t - 4) = 0$ \\
Check the middle term: $4t \times (-4) + 25 \times t = -16t + 25t = 9t$ \\
$t = -\frac{25}{4}$ or $t = 4$ \\
$t > 0$, so reject $t = -\frac{25}{4}$ \\
$t = 4$ \\
Find $\mathbf{r}$ at $t = 4$: \\
$2t^{2} = 2 \times 16 = 32$ \\
$t^{\frac{3}{2}} = (\sqrt{4})^{3} = 8$ \\
$4 \times 8 = 32$ \\
$\mathbf{j}$ component: $32 - 108 = -76$ \\
$\mathbf{r} = 32\mathbf{i} - 76\mathbf{j}$ \\
Distance from $O$ $= |\mathbf{r}|$ \\
$= \sqrt{32^{2} + (-76)^{2}}$ \\
$32^{2} = 1024$ \\
$76^{2} = 5776$ \\
$= \sqrt{1024 + 5776}$ \\
$= \sqrt{6800}$ \\
Pull out the square factor: $6800 = 400 \times 17$ \\
$\sqrt{6800} = 20\sqrt{17}$ \\
The distance is $20\sqrt{17}$ m \\
($20\sqrt{17} = 82.5$ m)

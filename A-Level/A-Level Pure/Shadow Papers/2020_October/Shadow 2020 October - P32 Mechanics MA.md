@q 1
@topic Brick on rough slope, limiting friction
@marks 9
@question
(a) $\tan \alpha = \frac{5}{12}$, so a right angled triangle has opposite 5, adjacent 12 and hypotenuse 13 \\
$\sin \alpha = \frac{5}{13}$ and $\cos \alpha = \frac{12}{13}$ \\
There is no motion perpendicular to the plane, so resolve perpendicular to the plane: \\
$R = mg\cos \alpha$ \\
$R = mg \times \frac{12}{13}$ \\
$R = \frac{12}{13}mg$ \\
(b) $B$ is on the point of sliding down, so friction acts up the slope and is at its maximum, $F = \mu R$ \\
Resolve parallel to the plane: \\
$F = mg\sin \alpha$ \\
$F = mg \times \frac{5}{13}$ \\
$F = \frac{5}{13}mg$ \\
Use $F = \mu R$: \\
$\frac{5}{13}mg = \mu \times \frac{12}{13}mg$ \\
Divide both sides by $\frac{12}{13}mg$: \\
$\mu = \frac{\frac{5}{13}mg}{\frac{12}{13}mg}$ \\
Cancel $\frac{mg}{13}$: \\
$\mu = \frac{5}{12}$ as required \\
(Also $\mu = \tan \alpha$) \\
(c) For brick $C$ of mass $M$, the normal reaction is $\frac{12}{13}Mg$ and the maximum friction is $\frac{5}{12} \times \frac{12}{13}Mg = \frac{5}{13}Mg$ \\
The weight component down the slope is $Mg\sin \alpha = \frac{5}{13}Mg$ \\
These are equal, because the mass cancels in the same way as for brick $B$ \\
So friction can just hold the brick, and brick $C$ stays at rest \\
(d) Brick $C$ is moving down the plane, so friction is the (kinetic) friction $\frac{5}{12}R$, which has the same size as the weight component down the slope \\
The forces down the plane balance, so there is no resultant force and no acceleration \\
So brick $C$ slides down the plane with constant speed (0.6 m s$^{-1}$)

@q 2
@topic Constant acceleration, position vector
@marks 8
@question
(a) Use $\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{u} + \mathbf{a}t$ with $\mathbf{u} = -2\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j}$, $\mathbf{a} = 5\mathbf{i} - 4\mathbf{j}$ and $t = 3$ \\
$\mathbf{a}t = 3(5\mathbf{i} - 4\mathbf{j}) = 15\mathbf{i} - 12\mathbf{j}$ \\
$\mathbf{v} = (-2\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j}) + (15\mathbf{i} - 12\mathbf{j})$ \\
$\mathbf{i}$ component: $-2 + 15 = 13$ \\
$\mathbf{j}$ component: $3 - 12 = -9$ \\
$\mathbf{v} = 13\mathbf{i} - 9\mathbf{j}$ m s$^{-1}$ \\
(b) $P$ starts at the origin, so the position vector is $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{u}t + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}t^{2}$ \\
$\mathbf{r} = (-2\mathbf{i} + 3\mathbf{j})t + \frac{1}{2}(5\mathbf{i} - 4\mathbf{j})t^{2}$ \\
$\mathbf{i}$ component: $-2t + \frac{5}{2}t^{2}$ \\
$\mathbf{j}$ component: $3t - 2t^{2}$ \\
At $t = T$ the position is $\lambda \mathbf{i} - 5\mathbf{j}$, so the $\mathbf{j}$ components are equal: \\
$3T - 2T^{2} = -5$ \\
Add 5 to both sides and rearrange to a positive $T^{2}$: \\
$0 = 2T^{2} - 3T - 5$ \\
Factorise: \\
$(2T - 5)(T + 1) = 0$ \\
$T = \frac{5}{2}$ or $T = -1$ \\
$T > 0$, so reject $T = -1$ \\
$T = 2.5$ \\
(c) Put $T = 2.5$ into the $\mathbf{i}$ component: \\
$\lambda = -2T + \frac{5}{2}T^{2}$ \\
$-2 \times 2.5 = -5$ \\
$T^{2} = 6.25$ \\
$\frac{5}{2} \times 6.25 = 15.625$ \\
$\lambda = -5 + 15.625$ \\
$\lambda = 10.625$ \\
$\lambda = \frac{85}{8}$

@q 3
@topic Integrating acceleration, speed of Q
@marks 12
@question
(i)(a) Velocity is the integral of acceleration: \\
$\mathbf{a} = (2 - 8t)\mathbf{i} + (5 - t^{2})\mathbf{j}$ \\
Integrate each component: \\
$\int (2 - 8t) dt = 2t - 4t^{2}$ \\
$\int (5 - t^{2}) dt = 5t - \frac{t^{3}}{3}$ \\
$\mathbf{v} = (2t - 4t^{2})\mathbf{i} + \left( 5t - \frac{1}{3}t^{3} \right)\mathbf{j} + \mathbf{c}$ \\
At $t = 0$, $\mathbf{v} = 156\mathbf{i}$, so $\mathbf{c} = 156\mathbf{i}$ \\
$\mathbf{v} = (2t - 4t^{2} + 156)\mathbf{i} + \left( 5t - \frac{1}{3}t^{3} \right)\mathbf{j}$ \\
Put $t = 5$: \\
$\mathbf{i}$ component: $2(5) - 4(25) + 156 = 10 - 100 + 156 = 66$ \\
$\mathbf{j}$ component: $5(5) - \frac{125}{3} = 25 - \frac{125}{3}$ \\
$25 = \frac{75}{3}$ \\
$\frac{75}{3} - \frac{125}{3} = -\frac{50}{3}$ \\
$\mathbf{v} = 66\mathbf{i} - \frac{50}{3}\mathbf{j}$ m s$^{-1}$ \\
(b) Moving perpendicular to $\mathbf{i}$ means the velocity is in the $\mathbf{j}$ direction only, so the $\mathbf{i}$ component is zero \\
$2t - 4t^{2} + 156 = 0$ \\
Multiply by $-1$ and reorder: \\
$4t^{2} - 2t - 156 = 0$ \\
Divide by 2: \\
$2t^{2} - t - 78 = 0$ \\
Use the quadratic formula with $a = 2$, $b = -1$, $c = -78$: \\
$t = \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{1 + 624}}{4}$ \\
$t = \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{625}}{4}$ \\
$t = \frac{1 \pm 25}{4}$ \\
$t = 6.5$ or $t = -6$ \\
$t \geq 0$, so reject $t = -6$ \\
$t = 6.5$ seconds \\
(ii) The velocity is the derivative of the position vector: \\
$\mathbf{r} = (2t^{2} - 2t)\mathbf{i} + 5t\mathbf{j}$ \\
$\mathbf{v} = (4t - 2)\mathbf{i} + 5\mathbf{j}$ \\
Speed is the magnitude of the velocity: \\
$\text{speed} = \sqrt{(4t - 2)^{2} + 5^{2}}$ \\
Set the speed to 13: \\
$\sqrt{(4t - 2)^{2} + 25} = 13$ \\
Square both sides: \\
$(4t - 2)^{2} + 25 = 169$ \\
Subtract 25: \\
$(4t - 2)^{2} = 144$ \\
Square root both sides: \\
$4t - 2 = \pm 12$ \\
Case 1: $4t - 2 = 12$ \\
$4t = 14$ \\
$t = 3.5$ \\
Case 2: $4t - 2 = -12$ \\
$4t = -10$ \\
$t = -2.5$, which is not allowed as $t \geq 0$ \\
$t = 3.5$ seconds \\
(Check by expanding: $16t^{2} - 16t + 4 + 25 = 169$ gives $16t^{2} - 16t - 140 = 0$, and $t = 3.5$ gives $196 - 56 - 140 = 0$)

@q 4
@topic Ladder against a smooth rail
@marks 10
@question
(a) $\sin \alpha = \frac{12}{13}$, so a right angled triangle has opposite 12, hypotenuse 13 and adjacent 5 \\
$\cos \alpha = \frac{5}{13}$ \\
The rail is smooth, so the force from the rail at $C$ is perpendicular to the ladder; call it $N$ \\
The distance $AC$ along the ladder: the height of $C$ is 12$a$, so \\
$AC = \frac{12a}{\sin \alpha}$ \\
$AC = 12a \times \frac{13}{12}$ \\
$AC = 13a$ \\
The weight $Mg$ acts at the middle of the ladder, which is $\frac{15a}{2} = 7.5a$ along from $A$ \\
Take moments about $A$ (the forces at $A$ have no moment): \\
Moment of $N$: $N \times 13a$ \\
Moment of the weight: $Mg \times 7.5a\cos \alpha$ (the horizontal distance from $A$ to the weight is $7.5a\cos \alpha$) \\
$13aN = 7.5aMg\cos \alpha$ \\
Divide by $13a$: \\
$N = \frac{7.5Mg\cos \alpha}{13}$ \\
Put in $\cos \alpha = \frac{5}{13}$: \\
$N = \frac{7.5 \times 5}{13 \times 13}Mg$ \\
$N = \frac{37.5}{169}Mg$ \\
Multiply the top and bottom by 2: \\
$N = \frac{75}{338}Mg$ as required \\
(b) Forces at $A$: a normal reaction $R$ upwards and a friction force $F$ horizontally, towards the right (limiting) \\
The force $N$ at $C$ is perpendicular to the ladder, so it makes angle $\alpha$ with the vertical: \\
Vertical component of $N$: $N\cos \alpha$ (upwards) \\
Horizontal component of $N$: $N\sin \alpha$ (to the left) \\
Resolve horizontally: \\
$F = N\sin \alpha$ \\
$F = \frac{75Mg}{338} \times \frac{12}{13}$ \\
$F = \frac{900Mg}{4394}$ \\
Resolve vertically: \\
$R + N\cos \alpha = Mg$ \\
$R + \frac{75Mg}{338} \times \frac{5}{13} = Mg$ \\
$\frac{75Mg}{338} \times \frac{5}{13} = \frac{375Mg}{4394}$ \\
$R = Mg - \frac{375Mg}{4394}$ \\
Write $Mg$ over 4394: $Mg = \frac{4394Mg}{4394}$ \\
$R = \frac{4394Mg - 375Mg}{4394}$ \\
$R = \frac{4019Mg}{4394}$ \\
The ladder is in limiting equilibrium: $F = \mu R$ \\
$\frac{900Mg}{4394} = \mu \times \frac{4019Mg}{4394}$ \\
Multiply both sides by $\frac{4394}{Mg}$: \\
$900 = 4019\mu$ \\
Divide by 4019: \\
$\mu = \frac{900}{4019}$ \\
$\mu = 0.2239$ \\
$\mu = 0.224$

@q 5
@topic Projectile from a cliff
@marks 11
@question
(a) Take $g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$ \\
Take $O$ as the origin, with up as positive \\
The ball lands at $A$, which is 150 m horizontally and 50 m below $O$ \\
Horizontal motion (constant speed): the horizontal speed is $U\cos 60$ \\
distance $=$ speed $\times$ time \\
$U\cos 60 \times t = 150$ \\
$\cos 60 = \frac{1}{2}$ \\
$\frac{1}{2}Ut = 150$ \\
Multiply by 2: \\
$Ut = 300$ \\
$t = \frac{300}{U}$ \\
Vertical motion: $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^{2}$ with $s = -50$, $u = U\sin 60$, $a = -9.8$ \\
$-50 = U\sin 60 \times t - 4.9t^{2}$ \\
Substitute $t = \frac{300}{U}$: \\
$U\sin 60 \times \frac{300}{U} = 300\sin 60$ \\
$300\sin 60 = 300 \times \frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ \\
$= 150\sqrt{3}$ \\
$4.9t^{2} = 4.9 \times \frac{90000}{U^{2}}$ \\
$-50 = 150\sqrt{3} - \frac{441000}{U^{2}}$ \\
Add $\frac{441000}{U^{2}}$ and add 50 to both sides: \\
$\frac{441000}{U^{2}} = 150\sqrt{3} + 50$ \\
$150\sqrt{3} = 259.81$ \\
$\frac{441000}{U^{2}} = 309.81$ \\
Multiply by $U^{2}$ and divide by 309.81: \\
$U^{2} = \frac{441000}{309.81}$ \\
$U^{2} = 1423.46$ \\
$U = \sqrt{1423.46}$ \\
$U = 37.73$ \\
$U = 37.7$ to 3 significant figures as required \\
(b) Using $U = 37.7$: the initial vertical speed is $37.7\sin 60$ \\
$\sin 60 = 0.86603$ \\
$37.7 \times 0.86603 = 32.65$ m s$^{-1}$ \\
At the greatest height the vertical speed is 0 \\
Use $v^{2} = u^{2} + 2as$ with $v = 0$, $u = 32.65$, $a = -9.8$: \\
$0 = 32.65^{2} - 2(9.8)h$ \\
$32.65^{2} = 1066.0$ \\
$0 = 1066.0 - 19.6h$ \\
Add $19.6h$: \\
$19.6h = 1066.0$ \\
$h = 54.4$ m above $O$ \\
The ground is 50 m below $O$, so the height above the ground is: \\
$54.4 + 50 = 104.4$ \\
The greatest height above the ground is 104 m \\
(c) Air resistance slows the ball down, so a bigger projection speed is needed to reach the same point \\
The new value of $U$ is greater than 37.7 \\
(d) One further refinement: allow for the wind \\
(Or model the ball with a size and spin, or use a more accurate value of $g$)

@topic Velocity, speed, acceleration, position
@marks 8
@question
(a) Put $t = 2$ into $\mathbf{v} = 6t^{2}\mathbf{i} - 9t^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{j}$ \\
$6t^{2} = 6 \times 4 = 24$ \\
$9t^{\frac{1}{2}} = 9\sqrt{2}$ \\
$\mathbf{v} = 24\mathbf{i} - 9\sqrt{2}\mathbf{j}$ \\
Speed is the magnitude of the velocity: \\
$\text{speed} = \sqrt{24^{2} + \left( 9\sqrt{2} \right)^{2}}$ \\
$24^{2} = 576$ \\
$\left( 9\sqrt{2} \right)^{2} = 81 \times 2 = 162$ \\
$\text{speed} = \sqrt{576 + 162}$ \\
$= \sqrt{738}$ \\
$= 27.17$ m s$^{-1}$ \\
(Exactly, $\sqrt{738} = \sqrt{9 \times 82} = 3\sqrt{82}$) \\
(b) Acceleration is the derivative of velocity: \\
$6t^{2} \to 12t$ \\
$-9t^{\frac{1}{2}} \to -9 \times \frac{1}{2}t^{-\frac{1}{2}} = -\frac{9}{2}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}$ \\
$\mathbf{a} = 12t\mathbf{i} - \frac{9}{2}t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathbf{j}$ m s$^{-2}$ \\
(c) Position is the integral of velocity: \\
$\int 6t^{2} \, dt = 2t^{3}$ \\
$\int -9t^{\frac{1}{2}} \, dt = -9 \times \frac{t^{\frac{3}{2}}}{\frac{3}{2}} = -6t^{\frac{3}{2}}$ \\
$\mathbf{r} = 2t^{3}\mathbf{i} - 6t^{\frac{3}{2}}\mathbf{j} + \mathbf{C}$ \\
At $t = 1$, $\mathbf{r} = 2\mathbf{i} - 4\mathbf{j}$: \\
$2(1)^{3} = 2$ and $6(1)^{\frac{3}{2}} = 6$ \\
$2\mathbf{i} - 4\mathbf{j} = 2\mathbf{i} - 6\mathbf{j} + \mathbf{C}$ \\
Add $6\mathbf{j}$ and subtract $2\mathbf{i}$ from both sides: \\
$\mathbf{C} = 2\mathbf{j}$ \\
$\mathbf{r} = 2t^{3}\mathbf{i} + \left( 2 - 6t^{\frac{3}{2}} \right)\mathbf{j}$ \\
Put $t = 4$: \\
$2t^{3} = 2 \times 64 = 128$ \\
$t^{\frac{3}{2}} = (\sqrt{4})^{3} = 8$ \\
$6 \times 8 = 48$ \\
$\mathbf{j}$ component: $2 - 48 = -46$ \\
$\mathbf{r} = 128\mathbf{i} - 46\mathbf{j}$ m

@q 2
@topic Truck on rough slope, held by force
@marks 10
@question
(a)(i) $\tan \alpha = \frac{4}{3}$, so a right angled triangle has opposite 4, adjacent 3 and hypotenuse 5 \\
$\sin \alpha = \frac{4}{5}$ and $\cos \alpha = \frac{3}{5}$ \\
Take $g = 9.8$. The weight is $10g = 98$ N \\
The truck is held by a horizontal force $X$, so first find $X$ from the forces perpendicular to the plane \\
The normal reaction is $R = 100$ N \\
The horizontal force $X$ points into the plane, so it has a component $X\sin \alpha$ perpendicular to the plane, pressing the truck on to the plane \\
Resolve perpendicular to the plane: \\
$R = 10g\cos \alpha + X\sin \alpha$ \\
$100 = 98 \times \frac{3}{5} + X \times \frac{4}{5}$ \\
$98 \times \frac{3}{5} = 58.8$ \\
$100 = 58.8 + 0.8X$ \\
Subtract 58.8: \\
$41.2 = 0.8X$ \\
Divide by 0.8: \\
$X = 51.5$ N \\
Resolve parallel to the plane. The weight component down the plane is $10g\sin \alpha = 98 \times \frac{4}{5} = 78.4$ N \\
The horizontal force has a component $X\cos \alpha$ up the plane: \\
$X\cos \alpha = 51.5 \times \frac{3}{5} = 30.9$ N \\
The truck is in equilibrium, so friction $F$ must make up the difference. Take $F$ up the plane: \\
$F + X\cos \alpha = 10g\sin \alpha$ \\
$F + 30.9 = 78.4$ \\
Subtract 30.9: \\
$F = 47.5$ N \\
(ii) $F$ came out positive for friction acting up the plane \\
So the frictional force acts up the plane \\
(b) With $X$ removed, $B$ slides down the plane \\
Resolve perpendicular to the plane: \\
$R = 10g\cos \alpha$ \\
$R = 58.8$ N \\
Friction when sliding: $F = \mu R$ \\
$F = 0.25 \times 58.8$ \\
$F = 14.7$ N \\
This acts up the plane \\
Equation of motion down the plane: \\
$10g\sin \alpha - F = 10a$ \\
$78.4 - 14.7 = 10a$ \\
$63.7 = 10a$ \\
Divide by 10: \\
$a = 6.37$ m s$^{-2}$

@q 3
@topic Two forces, direction, distance
@marks 9
@question
(a) The resultant force is $\mathbf{F}_{1} + \mathbf{F}_{2}$: \\
$(2\mathbf{i} - 3\mathbf{j}) + (\alpha \mathbf{i} + \beta \mathbf{j})$ \\
$= (2 + \alpha)\mathbf{i} + (-3 + \beta)\mathbf{j}$ \\
The particle starts from rest, so it moves in the direction of the resultant force \\
That direction is $6\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j}$, so the components are in the ratio $6 : 4$ \\
$\frac{-3 + \beta}{2 + \alpha} = \frac{4}{6}$ \\
Cross multiply: \\
$6(-3 + \beta) = 4(2 + \alpha)$ \\
Expand both brackets: \\
$-18 + 6\beta = 8 + 4\alpha$ \\
Subtract $6\beta$ from both sides and add 18: \\
$0 = 8 + 4\alpha - 6\beta + 18$ \\
$0 = 4\alpha - 6\beta + 26$ \\
$4\alpha - 6\beta + 26 = 0$ as required \\
(b) Put $\alpha = 4$: \\
$4(4) - 6\beta + 26 = 0$ \\
$16 - 6\beta + 26 = 0$ \\
$42 - 6\beta = 0$ \\
$6\beta = 42$ \\
$\beta = 7$ \\
Resultant force: $(2 + 4)\mathbf{i} + (-3 + 7)\mathbf{j}$ \\
$= 6\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j}$ N \\
Use $\mathbf{F} = m\mathbf{a}$ with $m = 5$: \\
$6\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j} = 5\mathbf{a}$ \\
$\mathbf{a} = \frac{1}{5}(6\mathbf{i} + 4\mathbf{j})$ \\
$\mathbf{a} = 1.2\mathbf{i} + 0.8\mathbf{j}$ m s$^{-2}$ \\
The particle starts at rest from $A$, so use $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{u}t + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{a}t^{2}$ with $\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{0}$ and $t = 10$: \\
$\mathbf{r} = \frac{1}{2}(1.2\mathbf{i} + 0.8\mathbf{j}) \times 10^{2}$ \\
$= 50(1.2\mathbf{i} + 0.8\mathbf{j})$ \\
$= 60\mathbf{i} + 40\mathbf{j}$ \\
This is the vector $\text{AB}$ \\
$AB = \sqrt{60^{2} + 40^{2}}$ \\
$60^{2} = 3600$ \\
$40^{2} = 1600$ \\
$AB = \sqrt{5200}$ \\
Pull out the square factor: $5200 = 400 \times 13$ \\
$AB = 20\sqrt{13}$ \\
$AB = 72.1$ m

@q 4
@topic Plank held by string, friction
@marks 11
@question
(a) The string is perpendicular to the rod and pulls up and to the left, so the horizontal component of the tension $T$ acts to the left \\
The other forces are vertical (weights, vertical reaction) except for friction \\
For the rod to be in equilibrium horizontally, the friction must balance the horizontal component of $T$ \\
So friction acts horizontally to the right \\
(b) Take moments about $A$ (the forces at $A$ then have no moment) \\
Moment of the tension: the string is perpendicular to the rod at $B$, which is $2x$ from $A$, so the moment is $T \times 2x$ \\
Moment of the rod's weight $2Mg$: it acts at the middle, which is $x$ from $A$; the horizontal distance is $x\cos \theta$, so the moment is $2Mg \times x\cos \theta$ \\
Moment of the child's weight $4Mg$: $C$ is $1.8x$ from $A$; the horizontal distance is $1.8x\cos \theta$, so the moment is $4Mg \times 1.8x\cos \theta$ \\
$2xT = 2Mgx\cos \theta + 4Mg \times 1.8x\cos \theta$ \\
$4 \times 1.8 = 7.2$ \\
$2xT = 2Mgx\cos \theta + 7.2Mgx\cos \theta$ \\
$2xT = 9.2Mgx\cos \theta$ \\
Divide by $2x$: \\
$T = 4.6Mg\cos \theta$ \\
$4.6 = \frac{23}{5}$ \\
$T = \frac{23}{5}Mg\cos \theta$ as required \\
(c) $\cos \theta = \frac{4}{5}$ \\
Let $R$ be the vertical force from the ground at $A$ \\
The tension is perpendicular to the rod, so it makes angle $\theta$ with the vertical and has a vertical component $T\cos \theta$ upwards \\
Resolve vertically: \\
$R + T\cos \theta = 2Mg + 4Mg$ \\
$R = 6Mg - T\cos \theta$ \\
Put in $T = \frac{23}{5}Mg\cos \theta$: \\
$R = 6Mg - \frac{23}{5}Mg\cos^{2} \theta$ \\
$\cos^{2} \theta = \frac{16}{25}$ \\
$\frac{23}{5} \times \frac{16}{25} = \frac{368}{125}$ \\
$R = 6Mg - \frac{368}{125}Mg$ \\
Write $6Mg$ over 125: $6Mg = \frac{750}{125}Mg$ \\
$R = \frac{750 - 368}{125}Mg$ \\
$R = \frac{382}{125}Mg$ as required \\
(d) $\sin \theta = \frac{3}{5}$ (since $\cos \theta = \frac{4}{5}$) \\
Resolve horizontally. The horizontal component of $T$ is $T\sin \theta$ (to the left) and the friction $F$ is to the right: \\
$F = T\sin \theta$ \\
$T = \frac{23}{5}Mg \times \frac{4}{5} = \frac{92}{25}Mg$ \\
$F = \frac{92}{25}Mg \times \frac{3}{5}$ \\
$F = \frac{276}{125}Mg$ \\
The rod is in limiting equilibrium, so $F = \mu R$: \\
$\frac{276}{125}Mg = \mu \times \frac{382}{125}Mg$ \\
Multiply both sides by $\frac{125}{Mg}$: \\
$276 = 382\mu$ \\
Divide by 382: \\
$\mu = \frac{276}{382}$ \\
Divide the top and bottom by 2: \\
$\mu = \frac{138}{191}$ as required

@q 5
@topic Projectile range, max height, refinement
@marks 12
@question
(a) Take $g = 9.8$ m s$^{-2}$ \\
Horizontal motion has constant speed $U\cos \theta$ \\
The projectile lands at $B$, 150 m away, after time $T$: \\
$U\cos \theta \times T = 150$ \\
Vertical motion: the projectile starts and ends on the ground, so the vertical displacement is 0 \\
Use $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^{2}$ vertically with $u = U\sin \theta$ and $a = -g$: \\
$0 = U\sin \theta \times T - \frac{1}{2}gT^{2}$ \\
Factorise out $T$: \\
$0 = T\left( U\sin \theta - \frac{1}{2}gT \right)$ \\
$T \ne 0$, so \\
$U\sin \theta = \frac{1}{2}gT$ \\
Multiply by 2 and divide by $g$: \\
$T = \frac{2U\sin \theta}{g}$ \\
Put this into the horizontal equation: \\
$U\cos \theta \times \frac{2U\sin \theta}{g} = 150$ \\
$\frac{2U^{2}\sin \theta \cos \theta}{g} = 150$ \\
Multiply by $g$ and divide by 2: \\
$U^{2}\sin \theta \cos \theta = 75g$ \\
$75 \times 9.8 = 735$ \\
$U^{2}\sin \theta \cos \theta = 735$ as required \\
(b) At the maximum height the vertical speed is zero, and the height is 20 m \\
Use $v^{2} = u^{2} + 2as$ vertically: \\
$0 = U^{2}\sin^{2} \theta - 2g(20)$ \\
$U^{2}\sin^{2} \theta = 40g$ \\
$40 \times 9.8 = 392$ \\
Equation 1: $U^{2}\sin^{2} \theta = 392$ \\
Equation 2 (from part (a)): $U^{2}\sin \theta \cos \theta = 735$ \\
Divide equation 1 by equation 2: \\
$\frac{U^{2}\sin^{2} \theta}{U^{2}\sin \theta \cos \theta} = \frac{392}{735}$ \\
Cancel $U^{2}\sin \theta$: \\
$\frac{\sin \theta}{\cos \theta} = \frac{392}{735}$ \\
$\tan \theta = \frac{392}{735}$ \\
Divide the top and bottom by 49: \\
$\tan \theta = \frac{8}{15}$ \\
A right angled triangle has opposite 8, adjacent 15 and hypotenuse $\sqrt{64 + 225} = 17$ \\
$\sin \theta = \frac{8}{17}$ \\
$\sin^{2} \theta = \frac{64}{289}$ \\
Put into equation 1: \\
$U^{2} \times \frac{64}{289} = 392$ \\
Multiply by $\frac{289}{64}$: \\
$U^{2} = \frac{392 \times 289}{64}$ \\
$392 \times 289 = 113288$ \\
$U^{2} = \frac{113288}{64}$ \\
Divide the top and bottom by 8: \\
$U^{2} = \frac{14161}{8}$ \\
$U^{2} = 1770.125$ \\
(The question paper prints $\frac{1416}{8}$, but the working gives $\frac{14161}{8}$, as in the mark scheme) \\
(c) With air resistance the projectile is slowed down all the way \\
To still land at $B$, the launch speed must be bigger \\
So $V$ is greater than $U$, since air resistance has to be overcome \\
(d) One further refinement: include the effect of the wind \\
(Or model the projectile with a size and shape, or allow for spin)

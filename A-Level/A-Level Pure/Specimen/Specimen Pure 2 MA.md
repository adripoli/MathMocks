$1 + 4\cos \theta + 3\cos^{2}\theta \approx 1 + 4\left( 1 - \frac{1}{2}\theta^{2} \right) + 3\left( 1 - \frac{1}{2}\theta^{2} \right)^{2}$ \\

Collect like terms: \\
$= 8 - 5\theta^{2} + \frac{3}{4}\theta^{4}$ \\

$169 - 16k > 0$ \\

$169 > 16k$ \\

$(8 - x)\ln x = 0$ \\

$\ln x = 0$ gives $x = 1$ \\

$-\ln x + \frac{8}{x} - 1 = 0$ \\
Add $\ln x$ and 1 to both sides: \\
$\frac{8}{x} = 1 + \ln x$ \\
Multiply both sides by $x$ and divide by $(1 + \ln x)$: \\
$x = \frac{8}{1 + \ln x}$ as required \\

$f'(3.5) = -\ln 3.5 + \frac{8 - 3.5}{3.5}$ \\

$= 0.033$ (positive) \\
$f'(3.6) = -\ln 3.6 + \frac{8 - 3.6}{3.6}$ \\
$= -1.2809 + 1.2222$ \\
$= -0.059$ (negative) \\

So the $x$ coordinate of $Q$ lies between 3.5 and 3.6 \\

@q 7
@topic Exponential DE, ln p graph, bacteria
@marks 12
@question
(a) The rate of increase of the area is proportional to the area: \\
$\frac{dp}{dt} = kp$ \\
Separate the variables: \\
$\frac{1}{p} \, dp = k \, dt$ \\
Integrate both sides: \\
$\int \frac{1}{p} \, dp = \int k \, dt$ \\
$\ln p = kt + c$ \\
Raise $e$ to the power of both sides: \\
$p = e^{kt + c}$ \\
Use $e^{m + n} = e^{m} \times e^{n}$: \\
$p = e^{c} \times e^{kt}$ \\
Let $a = e^{c}$, which is a constant: \\
$p = ae^{kt}$ as required \\
(b) Take natural logs of $p = ae^{kt}$: \\
$\ln p = \ln(ae^{kt})$ \\
$\ln p = \ln a + \ln(e^{kt})$ \\
$\ln p = \ln a + kt$ \\
$\ln p = kt + \ln a$ \\
This is a straight line with $\ln p$ on the vertical axis and $t$ on the horizontal axis \\
The gradient is $k$, so $k = 0.14$ \\
The vertical intercept is $\ln a$, so $\ln a = 3.95$ \\
$a = e^{3.95}$ \\
$= 51.9$ \\
$a = 52$ (2 sf) and $k = 0.14$ \\
(c) $p = ae^{kt} = a\left( e^{k} \right)^{t}$ \\
Let $b = e^{k}$: \\
$p = ab^{t}$ \\
$b = e^{0.14}$ \\
$= 1.1503$ \\
$b = 1.15$ (3 sf) \\
So $p = 52(1.15)^{t}$ \\
(d)(i) When $t = 0$, $p = ab^{0} = a$ \\
So $a = 52$ mm$^2$ is the area of the culture when it was first placed on the dish \\
(ii) Each hour $p$ is multiplied by $b = 1.15$ \\
So the area of the culture increases by 15 percent each hour \\
(e) The model says the area increases without limit, but the dish has a fixed size, so the area of the culture cannot grow beyond it

@q 8
@topic Rates of change, hemispherical bowl
@marks 7
@question
$V = \frac{1}{3}\pi h^{2}(75 - h)$ \\
Expand the bracket: \\
$V = \frac{75}{3}\pi h^{2} - \frac{1}{3}\pi h^{3}$ \\
$V = 25\pi h^{2} - \frac{1}{3}\pi h^{3}$ \\
Differentiate with respect to $h$: \\
$\frac{dV}{dh} = 50\pi h - \pi h^{2}$ \\
Chain rule: $\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{dV}{dh} \times \frac{dh}{dt}$ \\
Rearrange: $\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{dV}{dt} \div \frac{dV}{dh}$ \\
(a) For $0 \le h \le 12$, $\frac{dV}{dt} = 160\pi$ \\
When $h = 10$: \\
$\frac{dV}{dh} = 50\pi(10) - \pi(10)^{2}$ \\
$= 500\pi - 100\pi$ \\
$= 400\pi$ \\
$\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{160\pi}{400\pi}$ \\
Cancel $\pi$: \\
$= \frac{160}{400}$ \\
$= 0.4$ cm s$^{-1}$ \\
(b) For $12 < h \le 24$, the flow is $\frac{dV}{dt} = 300\pi$ \\
When $h = 20$: \\
$\frac{dV}{dh} = 50\pi(20) - \pi(20)^{2}$ \\
$= 1000\pi - 400\pi$ \\
$= 600\pi$ \\
$\frac{dh}{dt} = \frac{300\pi}{600\pi}$ \\
$= 0.5$ cm s$^{-1}$

@q 10
@topic Area under parametric curve, ln
@marks 9
@question
(a) $x = \ln(t + 2)$ and $t > -\frac{2}{3}$ \\
$t + 2 > -\frac{2}{3} + 2 = \frac{4}{3}$ \\
$\ln$ is an increasing function, so: \\
$x = \ln(t + 2) > \ln\frac{4}{3}$ \\
Domain: $x > \ln\frac{4}{3}$ \\
(b) Find the values of $t$ at the ends of the region: \\
When $x = \ln 2$: $\ln(t + 2) = \ln 2$, so $t + 2 = 2$ and $t = 0$ \\
When $x = \ln 4$: $\ln(t + 2) = \ln 4$, so $t + 2 = 4$ and $t = 2$ \\
$\frac{dx}{dt} = \frac{1}{t + 2}$ \\
Area of $R = \int y \, dx = \int y\frac{dx}{dt} \, dt$ \\
$= \int_{0}^{2} \frac{1}{t + 1} \times \frac{1}{t + 2} \, dt$ \\
$= \int_{0}^{2} \frac{1}{(t + 1)(t + 2)} \, dt$ \\
Partial fractions: \\
$\frac{1}{(t + 1)(t + 2)} = \frac{A}{t + 1} + \frac{B}{t + 2}$ \\
Multiply both sides by $(t + 1)(t + 2)$: \\
$1 = A(t + 2) + B(t + 1)$ \\
Let $t = -1$: $1 = A(1)$, so $A = 1$ \\
Let $t = -2$: $1 = B(-1)$, so $B = -1$ \\
$\frac{1}{(t + 1)(t + 2)} = \frac{1}{t + 1} - \frac{1}{t + 2}$ \\
Integrate: \\
$\int \left( \frac{1}{t + 1} - \frac{1}{t + 2} \right) dt = \ln(t + 1) - \ln(t + 2)$ \\
Apply the limits: \\
$\left[ \ln(t + 1) - \ln(t + 2) \right]_{0}^{2}$ \\
Upper limit $t = 2$: $\ln 3 - \ln 4$ \\
Lower limit $t = 0$: $\ln 1 - \ln 2 = 0 - \ln 2$ \\
Subtract: \\
$= (\ln 3 - \ln 4) - (0 - \ln 2)$ \\
$= \ln 3 - \ln 4 + \ln 2$ \\
Use $\ln a + \ln b = \ln(ab)$ and $\ln a - \ln b = \ln\frac{a}{b}$: \\
$= \ln\left( \frac{3 \times 2}{4} \right)$ \\
$= \ln\frac{6}{4}$ \\
$= \ln\frac{3}{2}$ as required

@q 11
@topic Arithmetic series, sum is a square
@marks 5
@question
The terms of an arithmetic sequence have a common difference $d$: \\
$T_{3} - T_{2} = T_{4} - T_{3}$ \\
$(5k - 10) - 2k = (7k - 14) - (5k - 10)$ \\
Simplify the left-hand side: $5k - 10 - 2k = 3k - 10$ \\
Simplify the right-hand side: $7k - 14 - 5k + 10 = 2k - 4$ \\
$3k - 10 = 2k - 4$ \\
Subtract $2k$ from both sides: \\
$k - 10 = -4$ \\
Add 10 to both sides: \\
$k = 6$ \\
$T_{2} = 2k = 12$ \\
$T_{3} = 5k - 10 = 20$ \\
$T_{4} = 7k - 14 = 28$ \\
The common difference is $d = 20 - 12 = 8$ \\
The first term is $a = T_{2} - d = 12 - 8 = 4$ \\
$S_{n} = \frac{n}{2}(2a + (n - 1)d)$ \\
$= \frac{n}{2}(2(4) + (n - 1)(8))$ \\
$= \frac{n}{2}(8 + 8n - 8)$ \\
$= \frac{n}{2}(8n)$ \\
$= 4n^{2}$ \\
$4n^{2} = (2n)^{2}$ \\
So the sum of the first $n$ terms is the square of the number $2n$, so it is a square number

@question
$\sin x + \cos y = 0.5$ \\
Differentiate both sides with respect to $x$: \\
$\frac{d}{dx}(\sin x) = \cos x$ \\
$\frac{d}{dx}(\cos y) = -\sin y\frac{dy}{dx}$ \\
$\cos x - \sin y\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$ \\
Rearrange for $\frac{dy}{dx}$: \\
$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{\cos x}{\sin y}$ \\
The tangent is parallel to the $x$-axis, so $\frac{dy}{dx} = 0$ \\
A fraction is zero when its numerator is zero: \\
$\cos x = 0$ \\
In the range $-\frac{\pi}{2} \le x < \frac{3\pi}{2}$: $x = -\frac{\pi}{2}$ or $x = \frac{\pi}{2}$ (as $\frac{3\pi}{2}$ is excluded) \\
Case $x = \frac{\pi}{2}$: \\
$\sin\frac{\pi}{2} + \cos y = 0.5$ \\
$1 + \cos y = 0.5$ \\
$\cos y = -0.5$ \\
In the range $-\pi < y < \pi$: $y = \frac{2\pi}{3}$ or $y = -\frac{2\pi}{3}$ \\
For both, $\sin y \ne 0$, so $\frac{dy}{dx}$ is defined and equal to 0 \\
Case $x = -\frac{\pi}{2}$: \\
$\sin\left( -\frac{\pi}{2} \right) + \cos y = 0.5$ \\
$-1 + \cos y = 0.5$ \\
$\cos y = 1.5$ \\
This has no solutions, as $\cos y \le 1$ for all $y$ \\
So there are exactly two points: \\
$P = \left( \frac{\pi}{2}, \frac{2\pi}{3} \right)$ or $P = \left( \frac{\pi}{2}, -\frac{2\pi}{3} \right)$

@q 13
@topic Identity proof, cosec 2x + cot 2x
@marks 10
@question
(a) Write in terms of $\sin 2x$ and $\cos 2x$: \\
$\cosec 2x + \cot 2x = \frac{1}{\sin 2x} + \frac{\cos 2x}{\sin 2x}$ \\
Combine over the common denominator: \\
$= \frac{1 + \cos 2x}{\sin 2x}$ \\
Use $\cos 2x = 2\cos^{2} x - 1$ on the top and $\sin 2x = 2\sin x \cos x$ on the bottom: \\
$= \frac{1 + 2\cos^{2} x - 1}{2\sin x \cos x}$ \\
The $+1$ and $-1$ cancel: \\
$= \frac{2\cos^{2} x}{2\sin x \cos x}$ \\
Cancel the common factor $2\cos x$: \\
$= \frac{\cos x}{\sin x}$ \\
$= \cot x$ as required \\
(b) Let $x = 2\theta + 5$ degrees, so that $2x = 4\theta + 10$ degrees \\
By (a): \\
$\cosec(4\theta + 10) + \cot(4\theta + 10) = \cot(2\theta + 5)$ \\
$\cot(2\theta + 5) = \sqrt{3}$ \\
Take the reciprocal of both sides: \\
$\tan(2\theta + 5) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ \\
$\tan^{-1}\left( \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}} \right) = 30$ degrees \\
$\tan$ repeats every 180 degrees: \\
$2\theta + 5 = 30 + 180n$ \\
Subtract 5 from both sides: \\
$2\theta = 25 + 180n$ \\
Divide both sides by 2: \\
$\theta = 12.5 + 90n$ \\
$n = 0$: $\theta = 12.5$ degrees \\
$n = 1$: $\theta = 102.5$ degrees \\
$n = 2$: $\theta = 192.5$ degrees, which is outside the range $0 \le \theta < 180$ \\
$\theta = 12.5$ degrees or $\theta = 102.5$ degrees

@q 14
@topic Proofs: 3^x vs 2^x, irrationality
@marks 8
@question
(i) Test some values of $x$: \\
$x = -1$: $3^{-1} = \frac{1}{3}$ and $2^{-1} = \frac{1}{2}$ \\
$\frac{1}{3} < \frac{1}{2}$, so $3^{x} \ge 2^{x}$ is false when $x = -1$ \\
$x = 2$: $3^{2} = 9$ and $2^{2} = 4$ \\
$9 \ge 4$, so $3^{x} \ge 2^{x}$ is true when $x = 2$ \\
(In general the claim is true for $x \ge 0$ and false for $x < 0$.) \\
The claim is true for some values of $x$ and false for others, so it is sometimes true \\
(ii) Assume the opposite: $\sqrt{3}$ is rational \\
Then $\sqrt{3} = \frac{p}{q}$, where $p$ and $q$ are integers, $q \ne 0$, and $p$ and $q$ have no common factor (the fraction is in its lowest terms) \\
Multiply both sides by $q$: \\
$p = \sqrt{3}q$ \\
Square both sides: \\
$p^{2} = 3q^{2}$ \\
So $p^{2}$ is a multiple of 3 \\
If $p^{2}$ is a multiple of 3, then $p$ is a multiple of 3 (as 3 is prime) \\
Let $p = 3c$, where $c$ is an integer \\
Substitute into $p^{2} = 3q^{2}$: \\
$(3c)^{2} = 3q^{2}$ \\
$9c^{2} = 3q^{2}$ \\
Divide both sides by 3: \\
$3c^{2} = q^{2}$ \\
So $q^{2}$ is a multiple of 3, and therefore $q$ is a multiple of 3 \\
Now $p$ and $q$ are both multiples of 3, so they have a common factor of 3 \\
This contradicts the assumption that $p$ and $q$ have no common factor \\
So the assumption is false, and $\sqrt{3}$ is irrational

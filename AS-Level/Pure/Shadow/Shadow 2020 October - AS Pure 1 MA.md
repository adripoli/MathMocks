@marks 6
@question
(a) The displacement is the final position minus the starting position: \\
$(-2\mathbf{i} - 7\mathbf{j}) - (6\mathbf{i} - \mathbf{j})$ \\
$\mathbf{i}$ part: $-2 - 6 = -8$ \\
$\mathbf{j}$ part: $-7 - (-1) = -7 + 1 = -6$ \\
Displacement $= -8\mathbf{i} - 6\mathbf{j}$ km \\
So the boat moves 8 km west and 6 km south, which is a direction between south and west \\
A bearing is measured clockwise from north. South is a bearing of 180 degrees and west is 270 degrees \\
Let $\alpha$ be the angle between south and the direction of travel, measured towards the west. In the right-angled triangle, the side opposite $\alpha$ is the westward distance 8 and the side next to it is the southward distance 6: \\
$\tan \alpha = \frac{8}{6}$ \\
Cancel the common factor 2: \\
$\tan \alpha = \frac{4}{3}$ \\
$\alpha = \tan^{-1}\left( \frac{4}{3} \right)$ \\
$\alpha = 53.1301...$ degrees \\
Bearing $= 180 + \alpha$ \\
$= 180 + 53.1301...$ \\
$= 233.1$ degrees (1 dp) \\
(b) The distance travelled is the magnitude of the displacement: \\
$\text{distance} = \sqrt{(-8)^{2} + (-6)^{2}}$ \\
$= \sqrt{64 + 36}$ \\
$= \sqrt{100}$ \\
$= 10$ km \\
The time from 09:00 to 12:15 is 3 hours 15 minutes. As a decimal, 15 minutes is $\frac{15}{60} = 0.25$ hours: \\
$\text{time} = 3.25$ hours \\
Use $\text{speed} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$: \\
$\text{speed} = \frac{10}{3.25}$ \\
$= 3.0769...$ \\
The speed is 3.08 km h$^{-1}$ (3 sf)

@q 3
@topic Surd equation and index equation
@marks 6
@question
(i) Simplify $\sqrt{20}$. Write 20 as $4 \times 5$: \\
$\sqrt{20} = \sqrt{4} \times \sqrt{5}$ \\
$= 2\sqrt{5}$ \\
The equation becomes: \\
$x\sqrt{5} - 2\sqrt{5} = 2x$ \\
Subtract $2x$ from both sides: \\
$x\sqrt{5} - 2\sqrt{5} - 2x = 0$ \\
Add $2\sqrt{5}$ to both sides, so the $x$ terms are together: \\
$x\sqrt{5} - 2x = 2\sqrt{5}$ \\
Factorise the left-hand side by taking out $x$: \\
$x\left( \sqrt{5} - 2 \right) = 2\sqrt{5}$ \\
Divide both sides by $\left( \sqrt{5} - 2 \right)$: \\
$x = \frac{2\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{5} - 2}$ \\
Rationalise the denominator by multiplying the top and bottom by $\left( \sqrt{5} + 2 \right)$: \\
$x = \frac{2\sqrt{5}\left( \sqrt{5} + 2 \right)}{\left( \sqrt{5} - 2 \right)\left( \sqrt{5} + 2 \right)}$ \\
Top: $2\sqrt{5} \times \sqrt{5} + 2\sqrt{5} \times 2 = 2 \times 5 + 4\sqrt{5} = 10 + 4\sqrt{5}$ \\
Bottom, using the difference of two squares: $\left( \sqrt{5} \right)^{2} - 2^{2} = 5 - 4 = 1$ \\
$x = \frac{10 + 4\sqrt{5}}{1}$ \\
$x = 10 + 4\sqrt{5}$ \\
(ii) Write both sides as powers of 3. Use $9 = 3^{2}$ on the left: \\
$9^{5x - 1} = \left( 3^{2} \right)^{5x - 1}$ \\
Multiply the indices: \\
$= 3^{2(5x - 1)}$ \\
$= 3^{10x - 2}$ \\
On the right, $3\sqrt{3} = 3^{1} \times 3^{\frac{1}{2}}$. Add the indices: \\
$3\sqrt{3} = 3^{\frac{3}{2}}$ \\
A reciprocal is a negative power: \\
$\frac{1}{3\sqrt{3}} = 3^{-\frac{3}{2}}$ \\
The equation is now: \\
$3^{10x - 2} = 3^{-\frac{3}{2}}$ \\
The bases are equal, so the indices are equal: \\
$10x - 2 = -\frac{3}{2}$ \\
Add 2 to both sides: \\
$10x = -\frac{3}{2} + 2$ \\
$10x = \frac{1}{2}$ \\
Divide both sides by 10: \\
$x = \frac{1}{20}$ \\
(Check: $9^{5 \times \frac{1}{20} - 1} = 9^{-\frac{3}{4}} = 3^{-\frac{3}{2}}$, as required)

(a) A linear model has the form $A = mn + c$ \\
In 1998, $n = 0$ and $A = 203$. So $c = 203$ \\
In 2008, $n = 10$ and $A = 153.5$. Find the gradient from the two points $(0, 203)$ and $(10, 153.5)$: \\
$m = \frac{153.5 - 203}{10 - 0}$ \\
$= \frac{-49.5}{10}$ \\
$= -4.95$ \\
$A = 203 - 4.95n$ \\
(Check with $n = 10$: $203 - 49.5 = 153.5$) \\
(b) 2019 is $2019 - 1998 = 21$ years after 1998, so $n = 21$: \\
$A = 203 - 4.95 \times 21$ \\
$= 203 - 103.95$ \\
$= 99.05$ \\

@q 5
@topic Sine rule ambiguous case, beam lengths
@marks 6
@question
(a) Use the sine rule in triangle $ABC$, $\frac{\sin ACB}{AB} = \frac{\sin BAC}{BC}$: \\
$\frac{\sin ACB}{14} = \frac{\sin 31}{9}$ \\
Multiply both sides by 14: \\
$\sin ACB = \frac{14 \sin 31}{9}$ \\
$= \frac{14 \times 0.515038}{9}$ \\
$= \frac{7.21053}{9}$ \\
$= 0.80117$ \\
$ACB = \sin^{-1}(0.80117)$ \\
$ACB = 53.24...$ degrees, or the other angle with the same sine: \\
$ACB = 180 - 53.24...$ \\
$= 126.76...$ degrees \\
Decide which is right. $BC = BD$, so triangle $BCD$ is isosceles and its base angles $BCD$ and $BDC$ must be acute (less than 90 degrees) \\
$ACD$ is a straight line, so $ACB + BCD = 180$ degrees. If $ACB = 53.24$ then $BCD = 126.76$, which is not acute. So reject it \\
So $BCD$ is acute and $ACB$ is obtuse: \\
$ACB = 126.8$ degrees (1 dp) \\
(b) The beams $AB = 14$, $BC = 9$ and $BD = 9$ are already whole metres. Find the length of $AD = AC + CD$ \\
Use the unrounded value $ACB = 126.758...$ degrees. Angles in triangle $ABC$ add up to 180 degrees: \\
$ABC = 180 - 31 - 126.758...$ \\
$= 22.242...$ degrees \\
Use the sine rule to find $AC$, $\frac{AC}{\sin ABC} = \frac{BC}{\sin BAC}$: \\
$AC = \frac{9 \sin 22.242...}{\sin 31}$ \\
$= \frac{9 \times 0.37852}{0.515038}$ \\
$= 6.614...$ m \\
Now $CD$. Angle $BCD = 180 - 126.758... = 53.242...$ degrees \\
In the isosceles triangle $BCD$, the perpendicular from $B$ to $CD$ cuts $CD$ in half. Half of $CD$ is $BC \cos BCD$: \\
$CD = 2 \times 9 \times \cos 53.242...$ \\
$= 18 \times 0.59844$ \\
$= 10.772...$ m \\
$AD = AC + CD$ \\
$= 6.614... + 10.772...$ \\
$= 17.386...$ m \\
Beams are bought in whole metres, so $AD$ must be 18 m \\
Total steel $= AB + BD + BC + AD$ \\
$= 14 + 9 + 9 + 18$ \\
$= 50$ m

@question
(a) Use the binomial expansion with first term 1, second term $kx$ and $n = 20$: \\
$(1 + kx)^{20} = 1 + 20C1(kx) + 20C2(kx)^{2} + 20C3(kx)^{3} + ...$ \\
The coefficients are: \\
$20C1 = 20$ \\
$20C2 = \frac{20 \times 19}{2} = 190$ \\
$20C3 = \frac{20 \times 19 \times 18}{3 \times 2 \times 1} = \frac{6840}{6} = 1140$ \\
Raise the bracket to its power in each term, $(kx)^{2} = k^{2}x^{2}$ and $(kx)^{3} = k^{3}x^{3}$: \\
$(1 + kx)^{20} = 1 + 20kx + 190k^{2}x^{2} + 1140k^{3}x^{3} + ...$ \\
(b) The coefficient of $x^{3}$ is $1140k^{3}$ and the coefficient of $x$ is $20k$: \\
$1140k^{3} = 19 \times 20k$ \\
$1140k^{3} = 380k$ \\
Subtract $380k$ from both sides: \\
$1140k^{3} - 380k = 0$ \\
Factorise by taking out $380k$: \\
$380k\left( 3k^{2} - 1 \right) = 0$ \\
$k = 0$ or $3k^{2} - 1 = 0$ \\
$k = 0$ is not allowed, as $k$ is non-zero. Reject it \\
$3k^{2} = 1$ \\
$k^{2} = \frac{1}{3}$ \\

$k = \pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}$ \\

@question

Upper limit $x = k$: $7\sqrt{k} + 2k$ \\
Lower limit $x = 1$: $7\sqrt{1} + 2(1) = 7 + 2 = 9$ \\
Subtract the lower limit from the upper limit: \\
$7\sqrt{k} + 2k - 9 = 21$ \\
Subtract 21 from both sides: \\
$2k + 7\sqrt{k} - 9 - 21 = 0$ \\
$2k + 7\sqrt{k} - 30 = 0$, as required \\

$2u^{2} + 7u - 30 = 0$ \\

$2u^{2} + 12u - 5u - 30 = 0$ \\
$2u(u + 6) - 5(u + 6) = 0$ \\
$(2u - 5)(u + 6) = 0$ \\
$u = \frac{5}{2}$ or $u = -6$ \\
$u = \sqrt{k}$ cannot be negative, so reject $u = -6$ \\
$\sqrt{k} = \frac{5}{2}$ \\

$k = \frac{25}{4}$ \\

@question
(a) The cup was placed on the table at $t = 0$: \\
$\theta = 16 + 69e^{0}$ \\

$\theta = 16 + 69$ \\
$\theta = 85$ degrees C \\
(b) Put $\theta = 40$: \\
$40 = 16 + 69e^{-\frac{t}{6}}$ \\
Subtract 16 from both sides: \\
$24 = 69e^{-\frac{t}{6}}$ \\
Divide both sides by 69: \\
$e^{-\frac{t}{6}} = \frac{24}{69}$ \\
$e^{-\frac{t}{6}} = \frac{8}{23}$ \\
Take natural logs of both sides: \\
$-\frac{t}{6} = \ln \left( \frac{8}{23} \right)$ \\
Multiply both sides by $-6$: \\
$t = -6\ln \left( \frac{8}{23} \right)$ \\
Use $-\ln \left( \frac{8}{23} \right) = \ln \left( \frac{23}{8} \right)$: \\
$t = 6\ln \left( \frac{23}{8} \right)$ \\
$= 6 \times 1.056052...$ \\
$= 6.3363...$ \\
$t = 6.34$ minutes (2 dp) \\
(c) $e^{-\frac{t}{6}}$ is always positive, so $69e^{-\frac{t}{6}} > 0$ and: \\
$\theta = 16 + 69e^{-\frac{t}{6}} > 16$ \\
As $t$ gets very large, $e^{-\frac{t}{6}}$ gets closer to 0 and $\theta$ gets closer to 16 but never reaches it. So $\theta$ is always above 16 degrees C and can never fall to 10 degrees C \\
(d) Use the point $(0, 98)$ in $\mu = A + Be^{-\frac{t}{6}}$: \\
$98 = A + Be^{0}$ \\
Equation 1: $A + B = 98$ \\
Use the point $(6, 55)$: \\
$55 = A + Be^{-1}$ \\
Equation 2: $A + Be^{-1} = 55$ \\
Subtract equation 2 from equation 1 to remove $A$: \\
$B - Be^{-1} = 98 - 55$ \\
$B\left( 1 - e^{-1} \right) = 43$ \\
Divide both sides by $\left( 1 - e^{-1} \right)$: \\
$B = \frac{43}{1 - e^{-1}}$ \\
Multiply top and bottom by $e$: \\
$B = \frac{43e}{e - 1}$ \\
$B = 68.024998...$ \\
From equation 1: \\
$A = 98 - B$ \\
$= 98 - 68.024998...$ \\
$= 29.975002...$ \\
As $t \to \infty$, $e^{-\frac{t}{6}} \to 0$, so $\mu \to A$. The asymptote is $\mu = A$: \\
$\mu = 98 - \frac{43e}{e - 1}$ \\
$\mu = 29.98$ (2 dp) \\
(Check: $29.975 + 68.025 e^{-1} = 29.975 + 25.025 = 55.0$)

$= 2(27) + 11(9) - 45 - 108$ \\
$= 54 + 99 - 45 - 108$ \\

$p - 6 = 11$ \\
$p = 17$ \\

$-3q = -108$ \\

$= -\frac{756}{3} - \frac{616}{3}$ \\

$= \frac{9a^{2} - 6ab + b^{2}}{ab}$ \\
The top is a perfect square, since $(3a - b)^{2} = 9a^{2} - 6ab + b^{2}$: \\

$= -9 - 1$ \\

@q 3
@topic Bearings, cosine rule, lighthouses
@marks 4
@question
(a) Find the angle $ACB$ at lighthouse $C$ \\
The bearing of $C$ from $A$ is 251 degrees. The bearing of $A$ from $C$ is the back bearing, which is 180 degrees less: \\
$251 - 180 = 71$ degrees \\
So from $C$, lighthouse $A$ is on a bearing of 071 degrees and lighthouse $B$ is on a bearing of 042 degrees. The angle between them is: \\
$ACB = 71 - 42$ \\
$= 29$ degrees \\
Use the cosine rule in triangle $ABC$, $AB^{2} = AC^{2} + BC^{2} - 2(AC)(BC)\cos C$: \\
$AB^{2} = 10.4^{2} + 20.3^{2} - 2(10.4)(20.3)\cos 29$ \\
$10.4^{2} = 108.16$ and $20.3^{2} = 412.09$ \\
$2 \times 10.4 \times 20.3 = 422.24$ \\
$AB^{2} = 108.16 + 412.09 - 422.24\cos 29$ \\
$AB^{2} = 520.25 - 422.24\cos 29$ \\
$\cos 29 = 0.874620...$ \\
$AB^{2} = 520.25 - 422.24 \times 0.874620$ \\
$= 520.25 - 369.299$ \\
$= 150.95...$ \\
Take the positive square root: \\
$AB = \sqrt{150.95...}$ \\
$= 12.286...$ \\
$AB = 12.3$ km (1 dp) \\
(b) A ship at sea can be pushed sideways by wind or by a tide or current. To follow the straight line from $A$ to $B$, the captain may need to steer on a different bearing to cancel this out

$y = \frac{1}{x + k}$ is the same curve moved $k$ units to the left (since $k$ is positive) \\
So the vertical asymptote is $x = -k$ and the horizontal asymptote is $y = 0$ \\
Where it meets the $y$-axis: $x = 0$ gives $y = \frac{1}{k}$, so the point $\left( 0, \frac{1}{k} \right)$ \\
Sketch: to the right of $x = -k$ the branch is above the $x$-axis, falling from very high beside the asymptote $x = -k$, through $\left( 0, \frac{1}{k} \right)$, and flattening towards $y = 0$. To the left of $x = -k$ the branch is below the $x$-axis, going from near $y = 0$ down to very far down beside the asymptote $x = -k$ \\
(b) Here $k = 9$, so the vertical asymptote is $x = -9$ \\
Find where the curve meets the line $y = 7$: \\
$\frac{1}{x + 9} = 7$ \\
Multiply both sides by $(x + 9)$: \\
$1 = 7(x + 9)$ \\
Divide both sides by 7: \\
$\frac{1}{7} = x + 9$ \\
Subtract 9 from both sides: \\
$x = \frac{1}{7} - 9$ \\
$x = \frac{1}{7} - \frac{63}{7}$ \\
$x = -\frac{62}{7}$ \\
From the sketch, the curve is at or below $y = 7$ to the left of the asymptote $x = -9$ (all of that branch is negative), and to the right of the point where it meets $y = 7$ (the curve falls as $x$ increases) \\
$x < -9$ or $x \geq -\frac{62}{7}$ \\

@q 5
@topic Exact area between a line and a curve
@marks 5
@question
Find where the curve meets the line $y = -8$: \\
$5x^{2} - 43 = -8$ \\
Add 43 to both sides: \\
$5x^{2} = 35$ \\
Divide both sides by 5: \\
$x^{2} = 7$ \\
$x = \sqrt{7}$ (taking the positive value, as $R$ is to the right of the $y$-axis) \\
$R$ lies between the line (above) and the curve (below), from $x = 0$ to $x = \sqrt{7}$: \\
$\text{Area} = \int_{0}^{\sqrt{7}} \left( -8 - \left( 5x^{2} - 43 \right) \right) dx$ \\
Work out the integrand, removing the bracket: \\
$-8 - 5x^{2} + 43$ \\
$= 35 - 5x^{2}$ \\
Integrate each term using $x^{n} \to \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}$: \\
$\text{Area} = \left[ 35x - \frac{5x^{3}}{3} \right]_{0}^{\sqrt{7}}$ \\
Upper limit $x = \sqrt{7}$. Use $\left( \sqrt{7} \right)^{3} = 7\sqrt{7}$: \\
$35\sqrt{7} - \frac{5 \times 7\sqrt{7}}{3}$ \\
$= 35\sqrt{7} - \frac{35\sqrt{7}}{3}$ \\
Write $35\sqrt{7}$ as $\frac{105\sqrt{7}}{3}$: \\
$= \frac{105\sqrt{7}}{3} - \frac{35\sqrt{7}}{3}$ \\
$= \frac{70\sqrt{7}}{3}$ \\
Lower limit $x = 0$ gives 0 \\
$\text{Area} = \frac{70\sqrt{7}}{3} - 0$ \\
$= \frac{70}{3}\sqrt{7}$ \\
So the area is $k\sqrt{7}$ with $k = \frac{70}{3}$

@q 6
@topic Circle not touching the y-axis
@marks 5
@question
(a) Complete the square on the $x$ terms and the $y$ terms. Half of 12 is 6 and half of 8 is 4: \\
$x^{2} + 12x = (x + 6)^{2} - 36$ \\
$y^{2} + 8y = (y + 4)^{2} - 16$ \\
Substitute into the equation: \\
$(x + 6)^{2} - 36 + (y + 4)^{2} - 16 + k = 0$ \\
Move the numbers to the right-hand side: \\
$(x + 6)^{2} + (y + 4)^{2} = 36 + 16 - k$ \\
$(x + 6)^{2} + (y + 4)^{2} = 52 - k$ \\
Compare with $(x - a)^{2} + (y - b)^{2} = r^{2}$: \\
The centre is $(-6, -4)$ \\
(b) The radius satisfies $r^{2} = 52 - k$ \\
The centre is 6 units from the $y$-axis (its $x$ coordinate is $-6$) \\
For the circle not to cut or touch the $y$-axis, the radius must be less than 6: \\
$r < 6$ \\
$r^{2} < 36$ \\
$52 - k < 36$ \\
Subtract 52 from both sides: \\
$-k < -16$ \\
Multiply both sides by $-1$ and reverse the inequality sign: \\
$k > 16$ \\
For the circle to exist, $r^{2} > 0$: \\
$52 - k > 0$ \\
$k < 52$ \\
$16 < k < 52$

@marks 8
@question
(a) A linear model has the form $V = md + c$ \\
Find the gradient from the two points $(350, 375)$ and $(1316, 30)$: \\
$m = \frac{30 - 375}{1316 - 350}$ \\
$= \frac{-345}{966}$ \\
Divide top and bottom by 69: \\
$= -\frac{5}{14}$ \\
So $V = -\frac{5}{14}d + c$ \\
Substitute $d = 350$ and $V = 375$: \\
$375 = -\frac{5}{14}(350) + c$ \\
$\frac{5}{14} \times 350 = 5 \times 25 = 125$ \\
$375 = -125 + c$ \\
Add 125 to both sides: \\
$c = 500$ \\
$V = 500 - \frac{5}{14}d$ \\
(Check with $d = 1316$: $\frac{5}{14} \times 1316 = 5 \times 94 = 470$, so $V = 500 - 470 = 30$) \\
(b)(i) At the start $d = 0$: \\
$V = 500 - 0$ \\
$V = 500$ litres \\
(b)(ii) The lorry runs out of fuel when $V = 0$: \\
$0 = 500 - \frac{5}{14}d$ \\
Add $\frac{5}{14}d$ to both sides: \\
$\frac{5}{14}d = 500$ \\
Multiply both sides by $\frac{14}{5}$: \\
$d = 500 \times \frac{14}{5}$ \\
$d = 100 \times 14$ \\
$d = 1400$ km \\
(c) The model predicts 1400 km and the lorry actually travelled 1388 km \\
The difference is $1400 - 1388 = 12$ km, which is less than 1% of 1400 \\
So the model is very accurate and is a good model for this lorry

@q 9
@topic Laws of logs into a quadratic form
@marks 5
@question
(a) Use the power law $n\log a = \log a^{n}$ on the second term: \\
$2\log_{b} (2x - 3) = \log_{b} (2x - 3)^{2}$ \\
The equation becomes: \\
$\log_{b} 15x - \log_{b} (2x - 3)^{2} = 1$ \\
Use the subtraction law $\log a - \log c = \log \frac{a}{c}$: \\
$\log_{b} \left( \frac{15x}{(2x - 3)^{2}} \right) = 1$ \\
Use the definition of a log, $\log_{b} N = 1 \Rightarrow N = b^{1}$: \\
$\frac{15x}{(2x - 3)^{2}} = b$ \\
Multiply both sides by $(2x - 3)^{2}$: \\
$15x = b(2x - 3)^{2}$ \\
Expand the bracket: \\
$(2x - 3)^{2} = 4x^{2} - 12x + 9$ \\
$15x = b\left( 4x^{2} - 12x + 9 \right)$ \\
This is the form $kx = b\left( px^{2} + qx + r \right)$ with $k = 15$, $p = 4$, $q = -12$ and $r = 9$ \\
(b) Put $b = 5$: \\
$15x = 5\left( 4x^{2} - 12x + 9 \right)$ \\
Divide both sides by 5: \\
$3x = 4x^{2} - 12x + 9$ \\
Subtract $3x$ from both sides: \\
$0 = 4x^{2} - 15x + 9$ \\
Factorise. Two numbers that multiply to $4 \times 9 = 36$ and add to $-15$ are $-12$ and $-3$: \\
$4x^{2} - 12x - 3x + 9 = 0$ \\
$4x(x - 3) - 3(x - 3) = 0$ \\
$(4x - 3)(x - 3) = 0$ \\
$x = \frac{3}{4}$ or $x = 3$ \\
Check the values in the original equation. Both $15x$ and $(2x - 3)$ must be positive for the logs to exist \\
$x = \frac{3}{4}$: $2x - 3 = \frac{3}{2} - 3 = -\frac{3}{2}$, which is negative, so $\log_{5} (2x - 3)$ cannot be found. Reject it \\
$x = 3$: $15x = 45$ and $2x - 3 = 3$, both positive. $\log_{5} 45 - 2\log_{5} 3 = \log_{5} \frac{45}{9} = \log_{5} 5 = 1$. Accept it \\
$x = 3$

@q 11
@topic Exponential cooling of tea
@marks 6
@question
(a) When the tea was first made, $t = 0$: \\
$T = 25 + 60e^{0}$ \\
$e^{0} = 1$, so: \\
$T = 25 + 60$ \\
$T = 85$ degrees C \\
(b) The rate of cooling is found from $\frac{dT}{dt}$. The derivative of $e^{-0.04t}$ is $-0.04e^{-0.04t}$: \\
$\frac{dT}{dt} = 60 \times (-0.04)e^{-0.04t}$ \\
$\frac{dT}{dt} = -2.4e^{-0.04t}$ \\
$t$ is in seconds. Exactly 2 minutes is $2 \times 60 = 120$ seconds: \\
$\frac{dT}{dt} = -2.4e^{-0.04 \times 120}$ \\
$= -2.4e^{-4.8}$ \\
$e^{-4.8} = 0.008229...$ \\
$= -2.4 \times 0.008229...$ \\
$= -0.01975...$ \\
The rate is negative, so the tea is cooling, at 0.01975... degrees C per second, which is approximately 0.02 degrees C per second, as required \\
(c) As $t$ gets very large, $e^{-0.04t}$ gets closer to 0, so: \\
$T \to 25 + 60 \times 0$ \\
$T \to 25$ \\
The limit is 25 degrees C

@q 12
@topic Trig identity, tan equation, count roots
@marks 9
@question
(a) Start with the left-hand side. Use $\tan x = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x}$ on the first term: \\
$\tan x \cos x = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x} \times \cos x = \sin x$ \\
$\text{LHS} = \sin x - \sin x \cos^{2} x$ \\
Take out the common factor $\sin x$: \\
$= \sin x \left( 1 - \cos^{2} x \right)$ \\
Use $\sin^{2} x + \cos^{2} x = 1$, so $1 - \cos^{2} x = \sin^{2} x$: \\
$= \sin x \times \sin^{2} x$ \\
$= \sin^{3} x$ \\
On the right-hand side, dividing by $\tan x$ is the same as multiplying by $\frac{\cos x}{\sin x}$: \\
$\text{RHS} = \frac{\left( \sin x + \cos^{3} x \right)\cos x}{\sin x}$ \\
The equation is now: \\
$\sin^{3} x = \frac{\left( \sin x + \cos^{3} x \right)\cos x}{\sin x}$ \\
Multiply both sides by $\sin x$ (not zero, as $\tan x \neq 0$): \\
$\sin^{4} x = \left( \sin x + \cos^{3} x \right)\cos x$ \\
Expand the bracket: \\
$\sin^{4} x = \sin x \cos x + \cos^{4} x$ \\
Subtract $\cos^{4} x$ from both sides: \\
$\sin^{4} x - \cos^{4} x = \sin x \cos x$ \\
The left-hand side is a difference of two squares, $\left( \sin^{2} x \right)^{2} - \left( \cos^{2} x \right)^{2}$: \\
$\left( \sin^{2} x - \cos^{2} x \right)\left( \sin^{2} x + \cos^{2} x \right) = \sin x \cos x$ \\
Use $\sin^{2} x + \cos^{2} x = 1$: \\
$\sin^{2} x - \cos^{2} x = \sin x \cos x$ \\
Divide every term by $\cos^{2} x$ (not zero, as $\tan x$ exists): \\
$\frac{\sin^{2} x}{\cos^{2} x} - 1 = \frac{\sin x}{\cos x}$ \\
$\tan^{2} x - 1 = \tan x$ \\
Subtract $\tan x$ from both sides: \\
$\tan^{2} x - \tan x - 1 = 0$, as required \\
(b) The equation is the one in part (a), so solve $\tan^{2} x - \tan x - 1 = 0$ \\
Let $T = \tan x$. Use the quadratic formula with $a = 1$, $b = -1$, $c = -1$: \\
$T = \frac{-(-1) \pm \sqrt{(-1)^{2} - 4(1)(-1)}}{2(1)}$ \\
$= \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{1 + 4}}{2}$ \\
$= \frac{1 \pm \sqrt{5}}{2}$ \\
$\tan x = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2} = 1.6180...$ or $\tan x = \frac{1 - \sqrt{5}}{2} = -0.6180...$ \\
For $-90 < x < 90$ degrees, each value of $\tan x$ gives exactly one $x$: \\
$x = \tan^{-1}(1.6180...) = 58.28...$ degrees \\
$x = \tan^{-1}(-0.6180...) = -31.71...$ degrees \\
$x = 58.3$ degrees or $x = -31.7$ degrees (1 dp) \\
(c) The equation has the same form with $x$ replaced by $\frac{1}{2}x$, so it is true exactly when $\tan \frac{1}{2}x = \frac{1 + \sqrt{5}}{2}$ or $\tan \frac{1}{2}x = \frac{1 - \sqrt{5}}{2}$ \\
The interval $-900 < x < 900$ means $-450 < \frac{1}{2}x < 450$ degrees \\
This interval has a length of 900 degrees. The graph of $\tan$ repeats every 180 degrees, and $900 = 5 \times 180$, so the interval holds 5 full periods of tan \\
In each period, $\tan \frac{1}{2}x$ takes each value exactly once \\
So each of the two values of $\tan \frac{1}{2}x$ gives 5 solutions, and none lands on the ends or on a value where $\tan \frac{1}{2}x$ does not exist \\
Number of solutions $= 2 \times 5$ \\
$= 10$

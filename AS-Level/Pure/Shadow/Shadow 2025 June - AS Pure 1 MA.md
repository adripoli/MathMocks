@question

$P = -(100)^{2} + 140(100) - 4690$ \\
$= -10000 + 14000 - 4690$ \\
$= 4000 - 4690$ \\
$= -690$ \\
$P$ is in thousands of pounds, so this is a loss of GBP 690000. A selling price of GBP 100 would make a loss, so it is not sensible \\
(b) A profit of more than GBP 150000 means $P > 150$ (in thousands): \\
$-x^{2} + 140x - 4690 > 150$ \\
Subtract 150 from both sides: \\
$-x^{2} + 140x - 4840 > 0$ \\
Multiply both sides by $-1$ and reverse the inequality sign: \\
$x^{2} - 140x + 4840 < 0$ \\
Find the roots of $x^{2} - 140x + 4840 = 0$ using the quadratic formula with $a = 1$, $b = -140$, $c = 4840$: \\
$x = \frac{140 \pm \sqrt{(-140)^{2} - 4(1)(4840)}}{2(1)}$ \\
$= \frac{140 \pm \sqrt{19600 - 19360}}{2}$ \\
$= \frac{140 \pm \sqrt{240}}{2}$ \\
$\sqrt{240} = 15.4919...$ \\
$x = \frac{140 - 15.4919...}{2} = 62.254...$ or $x = \frac{140 + 15.4919...}{2} = 77.745...$ \\
The U-shaped graph of $x^{2} - 140x + 4840$ is below zero between its roots: \\
$62.254... < x < 77.745...$ \\

@q 10

@marks 5
@question
Use the power law $n\log a = \log a^{n}$ on the left-hand side: \\
$2\log_{2} (x + 3) = \log_{2} (x + 3)^{2}$ \\
Write the 2 as a log to base 2. $2^{2} = 4$, so $2 = \log_{2} 4$: \\
$2 + \log_{2} (x + 6) = \log_{2} 4 + \log_{2} (x + 6)$ \\
Use the product law $\log a + \log b = \log (ab)$: \\
$= \log_{2} 4(x + 6)$ \\
The equation becomes: \\
$\log_{2} (x + 3)^{2} = \log_{2} 4(x + 6)$ \\
The logs have the same base, so the insides are equal: \\
$(x + 3)^{2} = 4(x + 6)$ \\
Expand both sides: \\
$x^{2} + 6x + 9 = 4x + 24$ \\
Subtract $4x + 24$ from both sides: \\
$x^{2} + 2x - 15 = 0$ \\
Factorise. Two numbers that multiply to $-15$ and add to 2 are 5 and $-3$: \\
$(x + 5)(x - 3) = 0$ \\
$x = -5$ or $x = 3$ \\
Check the values. $\log_{2} (x + 3)$ and $\log_{2} (x + 6)$ need $x + 3 > 0$ and $x + 6 > 0$, so $x > -3$ \\
$x = -5$ gives $x + 3 = -2$, which is negative, so $\log_{2} (-2)$ cannot be found. Reject it \\
$x = 3$: $2\log_{2} 6 = \log_{2} 36$ and $2 + \log_{2} 9 = \log_{2} 4 + \log_{2} 9 = \log_{2} 36$. Accept it \\
$x = 3$

@q 11
@topic Minimise area of a planter
@marks 13
@question
(a) The cross-section is a semicircle of radius $r$, so its area is $\frac{1}{2}\pi r^{2}$. The capacity is this area times the length $l$: \\
$\frac{1}{2}\pi r^{2}l = 75000\pi$ \\
Divide both sides by $\pi$: \\
$\frac{1}{2}r^{2}l = 75000$ \\
Multiply both sides by 2: \\
$r^{2}l = 150000$ \\
Divide both sides by $r^{2}$: \\
$l = \frac{150000}{r^{2}}$ \\
The fibreglass is the curved surface plus the two semicircular ends (the top is open) \\
The curved surface is half of the curved surface of a full cylinder, which is $2\pi rl$: \\
$\text{curved surface} = \frac{1}{2} \times 2\pi rl = \pi rl$ \\
The two semicircular ends together make one full circle: \\
$\text{ends} = 2 \times \frac{1}{2}\pi r^{2} = \pi r^{2}$ \\
$A = \pi rl + \pi r^{2}$ \\
Substitute $l = \frac{150000}{r^{2}}$: \\
$\pi rl = \pi r \times \frac{150000}{r^{2}}$ \\
Cancel one $r$: \\
$= \frac{150000\pi}{r}$ \\
$A = \frac{150000\pi}{r} + \pi r^{2}$, as required \\
(b) Write the first term as a power of $r$: \\
$A = 150000\pi r^{-1} + \pi r^{2}$ \\
Differentiate, using $\frac{d}{dr}(r^{n}) = nr^{n-1}$: \\
$\frac{dA}{dr} = -150000\pi r^{-2} + 2\pi r$ \\
$\frac{dA}{dr} = -\frac{150000\pi}{r^{2}} + 2\pi r$ \\
At a stationary point, $\frac{dA}{dr} = 0$: \\
$2\pi r = \frac{150000\pi}{r^{2}}$ \\
Divide both sides by $\pi$: \\
$2r = \frac{150000}{r^{2}}$ \\
Multiply both sides by $r^{2}$: \\
$2r^{3} = 150000$ \\
Divide both sides by 2: \\
$r^{3} = 75000$ \\
Take the cube root: \\
$r = \sqrt[3]{75000}$ \\
$r = 42.17...$ cm \\
(c) Differentiate $\frac{dA}{dr} = -150000\pi r^{-2} + 2\pi r$ again: \\
$\frac{d^{2}A}{dr^{2}} = 300000\pi r^{-3} + 2\pi$ \\
$\frac{d^{2}A}{dr^{2}} = \frac{300000\pi}{r^{3}} + 2\pi$ \\
$r$ is positive, so both terms are positive and: \\
$\frac{d^{2}A}{dr^{2}} > 0$ \\
A positive second derivative at a stationary point means a minimum, so $r = \sqrt[3]{75000}$ gives the minimum value of $A$ \\
(d) At the stationary point, multiply $2\pi r = \frac{150000\pi}{r^{2}}$ by $r$: \\
$2\pi r^{2} = \frac{150000\pi}{r}$ \\
So the minimum area is: \\
$A = \frac{150000\pi}{r} + \pi r^{2}$ \\
$= 2\pi r^{2} + \pi r^{2}$ \\
$= 3\pi r^{2}$ \\
$r^{2} = 75000^{\frac{2}{3}} = 1778.44...$ \\
$A = 3\pi \times 1778.44...$ \\
$= 16761.4...$ cm$^{2}$ \\
Convert to square metres. 1 m$^{2}$ is 10000 cm$^{2}$: \\
$A = \frac{16761.4...}{10000}$ \\
$= 1.67614...$ m$^{2}$ \\
Cost $= 20 \times 1.67614...$ \\
$= 33.52...$ \\
The minimum cost to paint one planter is GBP 33.52 \\
(e) One assumption: the paint is spread evenly with no wastage, so the cost is exactly proportional to the area of the outer faces. (Another: the outer faces are exactly the fibreglass area $A$, with the thickness ignored)

@question
(a) The gradient function is $\frac{dy}{dx}$. Read the gradient of $C$ from left to right: \\
To the left of the $y$-axis, $C$ is falling, so the gradient is negative \\
At the minimum on the $y$-axis the gradient is 0, so the gradient graph crosses the $x$-axis at the origin, going from below to above \\
Between the minimum and the maximum, $C$ is rising, so the gradient is positive \\
At the maximum the gradient is 0, so the gradient graph crosses the $x$-axis at the $x$ coordinate of the maximum \\
To the right of the maximum, $C$ is falling, so the gradient is negative \\
Sketch: an upside-down U-shaped curve (a negative quadratic). It starts below the $x$-axis on the left, crosses the $x$-axis at $x = 0$, rises above the axis to a highest point between the two crossings, comes back down and crosses the $x$-axis at the $x$ coordinate of the maximum of $C$, then continues below the axis. (b) The correct equation is: \\
$\frac{dy}{dx} = -3x^{2} + 6x$ \\
Justification. Rewrite it in factorised form: \\
$-3x^{2} + 6x = -3x(x - 2)$ \\
It is zero at $x = 0$ and at $x = 2$. This matches a minimum on the $y$-axis ($x = 0$) and a maximum at a positive $x$ coordinate ($x = 2$, in quadrant 1) \\
The $x^{2}$ coefficient is negative, so the gradient is negative for large positive and large negative $x$. This matches $C$ falling at both ends \\
Why the other three are wrong: \\
$\frac{dy}{dx} = -(x - 1)^{2} + 3$ is zero where $(x - 1)^{2} = 3$, so $x = 1 \pm \sqrt{3}$, which is not at $x = 0$. So there is no turning point on the $y$-axis \\
$\frac{dy}{dx} = x^{2} - 3x = x(x - 3)$ has a positive $x^{2}$ coefficient, so the gradient is positive for large negative $x$. But $C$ is falling on the left. Wrong shape \\
$\frac{dy}{dx} = -3x(x + 4)$ is zero at $x = 0$ and $x = -4$, so the second turning point would be at a negative $x$ coordinate, but the maximum of $C$ is at a positive $x$ coordinate

@q 15
@topic Prime statements, always or sometimes
@marks 4
@question
(a) Factorise by taking out $n$: \\
$n^{3} + 3n = n\left( n^{2} + 3 \right)$ \\
A prime number has exactly two factors, 1 and itself \\
For $n = 1$: \\
$1^{3} + 3(1) = 4 = 2 \times 2$, which is not prime \\
For $n \geq 2$: both $n$ and $n^{2} + 3$ are at least 2, so $n^{3} + 3n$ is a product of two numbers that are each more than 1. So it is not prime \\
So $n^{3} + 3n$ is never prime for $n \in \mathbb{N}$ \\
Statement (I) is never true \\
(b) Factorise by taking out $n$: \\
$n^{2} + 6n = n(n + 6)$ \\
For $n = 1$: \\
$1^{2} + 6(1) = 7$, which is prime. So the statement is true for $n = 1$ \\
For $n = 2$: \\
$2^{2} + 6(2) = 4 + 12 = 16 = 2 \times 8$, which is not prime. So the statement is false for $n = 2$ \\
(In fact for every $n \geq 2$ both $n$ and $n + 6$ are more than 1, so it is not prime) \\
It is true for some values of $n$ and false for others \\
Statement (II) is sometimes true

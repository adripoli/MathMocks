@question
The cubic equation $3x^3+5x^2-2x+6=0$ has roots $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$. Without solving the equation, find the cubic equation whose roots are $(2\alpha-1)$, $(2\beta-1)$ and $(2\gamma-1)$, giving your answer in the form $pw^3+qw^2+rw+s=0$, where $p$, $q$, $r$ and $s$ are integers. (5)
@scheme
$w=2x-1$, so $x=\frac{w+1}{2}$. B1 3.1a \\
Substitute $x=\frac{w+1}{2}$ into the original cubic. M1 3.1a \\
$\frac{3}{8}(w+1)^3+\frac{5}{4}(w+1)^2-(w+1)+6=0$. M1 1.1b \\
Expand and collect coefficients; at least two of the three non-leading coefficients correct. A1 1.1b \\
$3w^3+19w^2+21w+53=0$. A1 1.1b (5) \\
Notes: \\
B1: Connects $x$ and $w$. \\
M1: Substitutes into the cubic. \\
M1: Expands to a cubic in $w$. \\
A1: At least two coefficients correct. \\
A1: Fully correct equation in $w$.
@model
Let $w=2x-1$ for any of the original roots. \\
Then $x=\frac{w+1}{2}$. \\
Substitute this in $3x^3+5x^2-2x+6=0$: \\
$3(\frac{w+1}{2})^3+5(\frac{w+1}{2})^2-2(\frac{w+1}{2})+6=0$. \\
Multiply through by 8: \\
$3(w+1)^3+10(w+1)^2-8(w+1)+48=0$. \\
Expand the powers: \\
$3(w^3+3w^2+3w+1)+10(w^2+2w+1)-8w-8+48=0$. \\
Collect like terms: \\
$3w^3+19w^2+21w+53=0$. \\
Each transformed root therefore satisfies this cubic.

@q 2
@topic Sums of powers and equations
@marks 9
@question
(a) Use the standard results for summations to show that, for all positive integers $n$, $\sum_{r=1}^{n}r(r-1)^2=\frac{1}{12}n(n+1)(n-1)(an+b)$, where $a$ and $b$ are integers to be determined. (5) \\
(b) Hence determine the value of $n$ that satisfies $\sum_{r=1}^{n}r(r-1)^2=\sum_{r=1}^{n}5r$. (4)
@scheme
(a) $r(r-1)^2=r^3-2r^2+r$. B1 1.1b \\
Use $\sum r^3=\frac{n^2(n+1)^2}{4}$, $\sum r^2=\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}$ and $\sum r=\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$. M1 A1 1.1b \\
$\sum r(r-1)^2=\frac{n(n+1)}{12}[3n(n+1)-4(2n+1)+6]$. dM1 1.1b \\
$=\frac{n(n+1)(n-1)(3n-2)}{12}$, so $a=3$, $b=-2$. A1 1.1b (5) \\
(b) $\frac{n(n+1)(n-1)(3n-2)}{12}=\frac{5n(n+1)}{2}$. M1 1.1b \\
$3n^2-5n-28=0$. A1 1.1b \\
$(3n+7)(n-4)=0$. M1 1.1b \\
As $n$ is positive, $n=4$. A1 2.3 (4) \\
Notes: \\
(a) B1: Correct expansion. M1: Uses standard summation formulae. A1: Correct substituted expression. dM1: Factors out $\frac{n(n+1)}{12}$. A1: Printed form with correct $a,b$. \\
(b) M1: Equates sums. A1: Correct equation. M1: Solves it. A1: Positive integer solution only.
@model
(a) First expand the summand: \\
$r(r-1)^2=r(r^2-2r+1)=r^3-2r^2+r$. \\
Therefore $\sum_{r=1}^{n}r(r-1)^2=\sum r^3-2\sum r^2+\sum r$. \\
Using the standard sums, this is \\
$\frac{n^2(n+1)^2}{4}-\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{3}+\frac{n(n+1)}{2}$. \\
Take out $\frac{n(n+1)}{12}$: \\
$\frac{n(n+1)}{12}[3n(n+1)-4(2n+1)+6]$. \\
Simplify the bracket: $3n^2+3n-8n-4+6=3n^2-5n+2$. \\
Factor it: $3n^2-5n+2=(n-1)(3n-2)$. \\
Thus $\sum_{r=1}^{n}r(r-1)^2=\frac{n(n+1)(n-1)(3n-2)}{12}$. \\
Hence $a=3$ and $b=-2$. \\
(b) The right side is $\sum_{r=1}^{n}5r=\frac{5n(n+1)}{2}$. \\
Equate the two sums: $\frac{n(n+1)(n-1)(3n-2)}{12}=\frac{5n(n+1)}{2}$. \\
Since $n$ is positive, divide by $n(n+1)$: $(n-1)(3n-2)=30$. \\
Expand: $3n^2-5n+2=30$. \\
Thus $3n^2-5n-28=0=(3n+7)(n-4)$. \\
The roots are $n=4$ and $n=-\frac{7}{3}$; only $n=4$ is a positive integer.

$(x-2)^2+(y-1)^2=(x+4)^2+(y+1)^2$. \\

@marks 9
@question
(i) The transformation $P$ is represented by the matrix with rows $(2,1)$; $(3,4)$. Determine the equation of the line of invariant points of $P$. (1) \\
(ii) The transformation $Q$ is represented by the matrix with rows $(3,-1)$; $(3,4)$. (a) Prove that, for the transformation $Q$, there are no invariant lines. (4) \\
The triangle $T$ has vertices at $(k,2)$, $(8,2)$ and $(8,4)$, where $k$ is a constant. Given that $T$ is transformed by $Q$ onto $T'$ and $T'$ has an area of 75, (b) determine the possible values of $k$. (4)
@scheme
(i) $P(x,y)=(x,y)$ gives $y=-x$. B1 1.1b (1) \\
(ii)(a) Let a candidate invariant line have direction $(1,m)$ and apply $Q$. M1 3.1a \\
$Q(1,m)=(3-m,3+4m)$, so invariance requires $3+4m=m(3-m)$. A1 1.1b \\
$m^2+m+3=0$. dM1 2.1 \\
Its discriminant is $1-12=-11<0$, so there is no real invariant direction or line. A1 2.4 (4) \\
(b) $\mathrm{det}(Q)=3(4)-(-1)(3)=15$. B1 1.1b \\
The original triangle has area $\frac{1}{2}|8-k|(4-2)=|8-k|$, so $15|8-k|=75$. M1 3.1a \\
$|8-k|=5$, giving one of $k=3,13$. A1 1.1b \\
Both $k=3$ and $k=13$. A1 1.1b (4) \\
Notes: \\
(i) B1: Correct fixed-point line. \\
(ii)(a) M1: Applies the matrix to a line direction. A1: Correct equations. dM1: Forms the quadratic. A1: Negative discriminant and conclusion. \\
(ii)(b) B1: Determinant. M1: Area scale. A1: One solution. A1: Both solutions.
@model
(i) A fixed point obeys $P(x,y)=(x,y)$. \\
The first coordinate gives $2x+y=x$, hence $y=-x$. \\
The second gives $3x+4y=y$, hence $3x+3y=0$ and again $y=-x$. \\
So the invariant points form the line $y=-x$. \\
(ii)(a) Every invariant line would have a real direction preserved by $Q$. \\
A nonvertical direction may be written $(1,m)$. \\
$Q(1,m)=(3-m,3+4m)$. \\
For this to be parallel to $(1,m)$, $3+4m=m(3-m)$. \\
Rearrange: $m^2+m+3=0$. \\
Its discriminant is $1^2-4(1)(3)=-11<0$, so it has no real slope. \\
A vertical direction $(0,1)$ maps to $(-1,4)$, which is not vertical. \\
Thus no real line is invariant under $Q$. \\
(ii)(b) The area scale factor is $|\mathrm{det}(Q)|=|3(4)-(-1)(3)|=15$. \\
The original triangle has base $|8-k|$ and height $4-2=2$. \\
Its area is $\frac{1}{2}|8-k|(2)=|8-k|$. \\
The image area is therefore $15|8-k|=75$. \\
Divide by 15: $|8-k|=5$. \\
So $8-k=5$ or $8-k=-5$, giving $k=3$ or $k=13$.

@marks 7
@question
$f(z)=z^4+pz^3+qz^2+rz-52$, where $p$, $q$ and $r$ are real constants. The roots of $f(z)=0$ are $\pm\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$, where $\alpha$ is real and positive. Given that $\beta=3+2\mathrm{i}$, (a) write down the root $\gamma$. (1) \\
(b) Determine the value of $\alpha$. (3) \\
(c) Hence determine the values of $p$, $q$ and $r$. (3)
@scheme
(a) $\gamma=3-2\mathrm{i}$. B1 1.2 (1) \\
(b) Product of roots is $-\alpha^2(3+2\mathrm{i})(3-2\mathrm{i})=-52$. M1 3.1a \\
$13\alpha^2=52$, so $\alpha^2=4$. M1 1.1b \\
Since $\alpha>0$, $\alpha=2$. A1 2.2a (3) \\
(c) $f(z)=(z-2)(z+2)(z-3-2\mathrm{i})(z-3+2\mathrm{i})$. M1 3.1a \\
$=(z^2-4)(z^2-6z+13)=z^4-6z^3+9z^2+24z-52$. A1 1.1b \\
$p=-6$, $q=9$, $r=24$. A1 1.1b (3) \\
Notes: \\
(a) B1: Conjugate root. \\
(b) M1: Product equation. M1: Solves for $\alpha^2$. A1: Positive value. \\
(c) M1: Correct factors. A1: At least two coefficients. A1: All coefficients.
@model
(a) The coefficients of $f$ are real, so nonreal roots occur in conjugate pairs. \\
Therefore $\gamma=3-2\mathrm{i}$. \\
(b) The product of the roots of the monic quartic is its constant term, $-52$. \\
$(\alpha)(-\alpha)(3+2\mathrm{i})(3-2\mathrm{i})=-52$. \\
The conjugate product is $3^2+2^2=13$. \\
Thus $-13\alpha^2=-52$. \\
Divide by $-13$: $\alpha^2=4$. \\
As $\alpha$ is positive, $\alpha=2$. \\
(c) Pair the real roots and conjugate roots: \\
$f(z)=(z-2)(z+2)(z-3-2\mathrm{i})(z-3+2\mathrm{i})$. \\
$=(z^2-4)[(z-3)^2+4]$. \\
$=(z^2-4)(z^2-6z+13)$. \\
Multiply: $z^4-6z^3+13z^2-4z^2+24z-52$. \\
Hence $f(z)=z^4-6z^3+9z^2+24z-52$. \\
Comparing coefficients gives $p=-6$, $q=9$, $r=24$.

@topic Divisibility by induction
@marks 6
@question
Prove by induction that, for $n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}$, $f(n)=6^{2n-1}+8^{n+1}$ is divisible by 7. (6)
@scheme
$f(1)=6+64=70$, divisible by 7. B1 2.2a \\
Assume $f(k)=6^{2k-1}+8^{k+1}$ is divisible by 7. M1 2.4 \\
$f(k+1)=36\cdot6^{2k-1}+8\cdot8^{k+1}$. M1 2.1 \\
$=28\cdot6^{2k-1}+8(6^{2k-1}+8^{k+1})$. A1 1.1b \\
$f(k+1)=28\cdot6^{2k-1}+8f(k)$ is divisible by 7. A1 1.1b \\
The base case and induction step prove the result for every positive integer $n$. A1 2.4 (6) \\
Notes: \\
B1: Base case. M1: Inductive assumption. M1: Forms $f(k+1)$. A1: Correct rearrangement. A1: Divisibility step. A1: Conclusion.
@model
For $n=1$, $f(1)=6^{1}+8^{2}=6+64=70=7(10)$. \\
So the statement holds at $n=1$. \\
Assume for some positive integer $k$ that $f(k)=6^{2k-1}+8^{k+1}$ is a multiple of 7. \\
Now $f(k+1)=6^{2(k+1)-1}+8^{(k+1)+1}$. \\
$=6^{2k+1}+8^{k+2}$. \\
$=36\cdot6^{2k-1}+8\cdot8^{k+1}$. \\
Split $36=28+8$: \\
$f(k+1)=28\cdot6^{2k-1}+8\cdot6^{2k-1}+8\cdot8^{k+1}$. \\
$=28\cdot6^{2k-1}+8(6^{2k-1}+8^{k+1})$. \\
$=28\cdot6^{2k-1}+8f(k)$. \\
The first term is divisible by 7, and the second is divisible by 7 by the assumption. \\
Hence $f(k+1)$ is divisible by 7. \\
By induction, $f(n)$ is divisible by 7 for every positive integer $n$.

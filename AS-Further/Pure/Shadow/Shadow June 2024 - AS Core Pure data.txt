@q 1
@topic Cubic roots and symmetric sums
@marks 9
@question
The cubic equation $3x^{3}+4x^{2}-2x+5=0$ has roots $\alpha$, $\beta$ and $\gamma$. \\
Without solving the equation, determine the exact value of \\
(i) $3\alpha^{2}+3\beta^{2}+3\gamma^{2}$ (3) \\
(ii) $\frac{2}{\alpha}+\frac{2}{\beta}+\frac{2}{\gamma}$ (3) \\
(iii) $(3-\alpha)(3-\beta)(3-\gamma)$. (3)
@scheme
No mark scheme for this question. See the Model tab (F3) for the full worked answer.
@model
By Vieta's formula, $\alpha+\beta+\gamma=-\frac{4}{3}$. \\
Also $\alpha\beta+\beta\gamma+\gamma\alpha=-\frac{2}{3}$ and $\alpha\beta\gamma=-\frac{5}{3}$. \\
(i) Use $(\alpha+\beta+\gamma)^{2}=\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}+2(\alpha\beta+\beta\gamma+\gamma\alpha)$. \\
$\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2}=\frac{16}{9}-2(-\frac{2}{3})$ \\
$=\frac{16}{9}+\frac{12}{9}=\frac{28}{9}$ \\
$3\alpha^{2}+3\beta^{2}+3\gamma^{2}=\frac{28}{3}$. \\
(ii) Use the reciprocal sum $\frac{1}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\beta}+\frac{1}{\gamma}=\frac{\alpha\beta+\beta\gamma+\gamma\alpha}{\alpha\beta\gamma}$. \\
$=\frac{-2/3}{-5/3}=\frac{2}{5}$ \\
Multiplying by 2 gives $\frac{4}{5}$. \\
(iii) Since $3x^{3}+4x^{2}-2x+5=3(x-\alpha)(x-\beta)(x-\gamma)$, set $x=3$. \\
$3(3-\alpha)(3-\beta)(3-\gamma)=3(27)+4(9)-2(3)+5$ \\
$=81+36-6+5=116$ \\
$(3-\alpha)(3-\beta)(3-\gamma)=\frac{116}{3}$.

@q 2
@topic Rotation and reflection matrices
@marks 10
@question
With respect to the right-hand rule, a rotation through $\theta$ degrees anticlockwise about the $y$-axis is represented by the matrix with rows $(\cos\theta,0,\sin\theta)$; $(0,1,0)$; $(-\sin\theta,0,\cos\theta)$. \\
Given that the matrix $M$ has rows $(-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2},0,\frac{1}{2})$; $(0,1,0)$; $(-\frac{1}{2},0,-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2})$ and represents a rotation through $\alpha$ degrees anticlockwise about the $y$-axis with respect to the right-hand rule, \\
(a) determine the value of $\alpha$. (1) \\
(b) Hence determine the smallest possible positive integer value of $k$ for which $M^{k}=I$. (2) \\
The $3\times3$ matrix $N$ represents a reflection in the plane with equation $z=0$. \\
(c) Write down the matrix $N$. (1) \\
The point $A$ has coordinates $(2,-1,4)$. The point $B$ is the image of $A$ under the transformation represented by matrix $N$ followed by the transformation represented by matrix $M$. \\
(d) Show that the coordinates of $B$ are $(-2-\sqrt{3},-1,-1+2\sqrt{3})$. (2) \\
Given that $O$ is the origin, \\
(e) show that, to 3 significant figures, the size of angle $AOB$ is $80.7$ degrees. (2) \\
(f) Hence determine the area of triangle $AOB$, giving your answer to 3 significant figures. (2)
@scheme
No mark scheme for this question. See the Model tab (F3) for the full worked answer.
@model
(a) Compare matrix entries: $\cos\alpha=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ and $\sin\alpha=\frac{1}{2}$. \\
Both signs place $\alpha$ in the second quadrant. \\
$\alpha=150$ degrees. \\
(b) Each use of $M$ adds a rotation of 150 degrees. \\
For $M^{k}=I$, $150k$ must be a multiple of 360. \\
Divide by 30: $5k$ must be a multiple of 12. \\
The least positive $k$ is $12$. \\
(c) Reflecting in $z=0$ changes only the sign of $z$. \\
$N$ has rows $(1,0,0)$; $(0,1,0)$; $(0,0,-1)$. \\
(d) First $N(2,-1,4)=(2,-1,-4)$. \\
Then multiply by $M$. \\
$x=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}(2)+\frac{1}{2}(-4)=-\sqrt{3}-2$. \\
$y=-1$. \\
$z=-\frac{1}{2}(2)-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}(-4)=-1+2\sqrt{3}$. \\
$B=(-2-\sqrt{3},-1,-1+2\sqrt{3})$ as required. \\
(e) Use the scalar product $\text{OA}\cdot\text{OB}=|\text{OA}||\text{OB}|\cos\phi$. \\
$\text{OA}\cdot\text{OB}=2(-2-\sqrt{3})+(-1)(-1)+4(-1+2\sqrt{3})$ \\
$=-7+6\sqrt{3}$. \\
Both transformations preserve length, so $|\text{OA}|=|\text{OB}|=\sqrt{2^{2}+(-1)^{2}+4^{2}}=\sqrt{21}$. \\
$\cos\phi=\frac{-7+6\sqrt{3}}{21}$. \\
$\phi=80.7$ degrees (3 sf), as required. \\
(f) Use the triangle area formula $\frac{1}{2}ab\sin C$. \\
$\mathrm{Area}=\frac{1}{2}(\sqrt{21})(\sqrt{21})\sin\phi$ \\
$=\frac{21}{2}\sin\phi=10.362097...$ \\
$\mathrm{Area}=10.4$ square units (3 sf).

@q 3
@topic Summations over a variable interval
@marks 10
@question
(a) Use the standard results for summations to show that, for all positive integers $n$, $\sum_{r=1}^{n}r^{2}(r+1)=\frac{1}{12}n(n+1)(n+2)(an+b)$ where $a$ and $b$ are integers to be determined. (4) \\
(b) Hence show that, for all positive integers $k$, $\sum_{r=3k+1}^{9k}r^{2}(r+1)=k(9k+1)(Ak^{2}+Bk+C)$ where $A$, $B$ and $C$ are integers to be determined. (3) \\
(c) Hence, using algebra and making your method clear, determine the value of $k$ for which $9\sum_{r=3k+1}^{9k}r^{2}(r+1)=1756k^{3}(9k+1)$. (3)
@scheme
No mark scheme for this question. See the Model tab (F3) for the full worked answer.
@model
(a) Expand $r^{2}(r+1)=r^{3}+r^{2}$. \\
Use $\sum_{r=1}^{n}r^{3}=\frac{n^{2}(n+1)^{2}}{4}$ and $\sum_{r=1}^{n}r^{2}=\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}$. \\
$\sum_{r=1}^{n}r^{2}(r+1)=\frac{n^{2}(n+1)^{2}}{4}+\frac{n(n+1)(2n+1)}{6}$ \\
$=\frac{n(n+1)}{12}[3n(n+1)+2(2n+1)]$ \\
$=\frac{n(n+1)}{12}(3n^{2}+7n+2)$ \\
$=\frac{n(n+1)(n+2)(3n+1)}{12}$ \\
Thus $a=3$ and $b=1$. \\
(b) Write $F(n)=\frac{n(n+1)(n+2)(3n+1)}{12}$. \\
The requested sum is $F(9k)-F(3k)$. \\
$F(9k)=\frac{9k(9k+1)(9k+2)(27k+1)}{12}$. \\
$F(3k)=\frac{3k(3k+1)(3k+2)(9k+1)}{12}$. \\
Factor $\frac{k(9k+1)}{12}$ from their difference. \\
The remaining bracket is $9(9k+2)(27k+1)-3(3k+1)(3k+2)$. \\
Expand: $9(243k^{2}+63k+2)-3(9k^{2}+9k+2)$. \\
$=2187k^{2}+567k+18-27k^{2}-27k-6$. \\
$=2160k^{2}+540k+12$. \\
Divide by 12: $F(9k)-F(3k)=k(9k+1)(180k^{2}+45k+1)$. \\
Thus $A=180$, $B=45$ and $C=1$. \\
(c) Substitute the result from part (b): \\
$9k(9k+1)(180k^{2}+45k+1)=1756k^{3}(9k+1)$. \\
As $k$ is positive, divide by $k(9k+1)$: \\
$9(180k^{2}+45k+1)=1756k^{2}$. \\
$1620k^{2}+405k+9=1756k^{2}$. \\
$136k^{2}-405k-9=0$. \\
Factor: $(k-3)(136k+3)=0$. \\
Since $k$ is positive, $k=3$.

@q 4
@topic Matrix inverse and simultaneous equations
@marks 8
@question
$A$ has rows $(1,-2,7)$; $(3,k,-2)$; $(-1,1,5)$ and $B$ has rows $(5k+2,17,4-7k)$; $(-13,12,23)$; $(3+k,1,k+6)$, where $k$ is a constant. \\
(a) Determine the value of the constant $c$ for which $AB=(12k+c)I$. (2) \\
(b) Hence determine the value of $k$ for which $A^{-1}$ does not exist. (2) \\
Given that $A^{-1}$ does exist, \\
(c) write down $A^{-1}$ in terms of $k$. (1) \\
(d) Use the answer to part (c) to solve the simultaneous equations $x-2y+7z=2$, $3x+ky-2z=1$, $-x+y+5z=-3$, giving the values of $x$, $y$ and $z$ in simplest form in terms of $k$. (3)
@scheme
No mark scheme for this question. See the Model tab (F3) for the full worked answer.
@model
(a) Calculate the top-left entry of $AB$. \\
$1(5k+2)+(-2)(-13)+7(3+k)$ \\
$=5k+2+26+21+7k$ \\
$=12k+49$ \\
The other diagonal entries agree and off-diagonal entries are zero. \\
Therefore $c=49$. \\
(b) The inverse fails when $12k+49=0$. \\
$12k=-49$ \\
$k=-\frac{49}{12}$. \\
(c) From $AB=(12k+49)I$, divide by $12k+49$. \\
$A^{-1}=\frac{1}{12k+49}B$, for $k\neq-\frac{49}{12}$. \\
(d) Write the equations as $A(x,y,z)=(2,1,-3)$. \\
Apply $A^{-1}$ to both sides: \\
$(x,y,z)=\frac{1}{12k+49}B(2,1,-3)$. \\
First component $=2(5k+2)+17-3(4-7k)$. \\
$=10k+4+17-12+21k=31k+9$. \\
Second component $=2(-13)+12-3(23)=-83$. \\
Third component $=2(3+k)+1-3(k+6)$. \\
$=6+2k+1-3k-18=-k-11$. \\
$x=\frac{31k+9}{12k+49}$, $y=\frac{-83}{12k+49}$, $z=\frac{-k-11}{12k+49}$.

@q 5
@topic Argand circle and segment area
@marks 10
@question
Given that on an Argand diagram the locus of points defined by $|z+7-24\mathrm{i}|=14$ is a circle, \\
(a) write down (i) the coordinates of the centre of this circle, (ii) the radius of this circle. (2) \\
(b) Show, by shading on an Argand diagram, the set of points defined by $|z+7-24\mathrm{i}|\leq14$. (1) \\
(c) For the set of points defined in part (b), determine the maximum value of $|z|$. (3) \\
The set of points $A$ is defined by $\{z:0\leq\mathrm{arg}(z+7-35\mathrm{i})\leq\pi\}$ intersected with $\{z:|z+7-24\mathrm{i}|\leq14\}$. \\
(d) Determine the area of the region defined by $A$, giving your answer to 3 significant figures. (4)
@scheme
No mark scheme for this question. See the Model tab (F3) for the full worked answer.
@model
(a) Compare $|z-c|=r$ with the given equation. \\
$z+7-24\mathrm{i}=z-(-7+24\mathrm{i})$. \\
Centre $=(-7,24)$ and radius $=14$. \\
(b) Draw the circle centred at $(-7,24)$ with radius 14. Shade the full interior, including its boundary. \\
(c) The centre is distance $\sqrt{(-7)^{2}+24^{2}}$ from the origin. \\
$=\sqrt{49+576}=25$. \\
The farthest point is one radius further out. \\
$|z|_{\mathrm{max}}=25+14=39$. \\
(d) The argument condition selects the half-plane above the horizontal line through $(-7,35)$. \\
Its intersection with the disc is the circular cap above $y=35$. \\
The centre is at height 24, so the line is distance $d=35-24=11$ above it. \\
Let $r=14$. The area of the circular segment is $r^{2}\cos^{-1}(d/r)-d\sqrt{r^{2}-d^{2}}$. \\
Substitute: $196\cos^{-1}(\frac{11}{14})-11\sqrt{196-121}$. \\
$=196\cos^{-1}(\frac{11}{14})-11\sqrt{75}$. \\
$=35.458689...$ square units. \\
$\mathrm{Area}=35.5$ square units (3 sf).

@q 6
@topic Three-dimensional lines and distance
@marks 12
@question
The drainage system for a sports field consists of underground pipes. This situation is modelled with respect to a fixed origin $O$. \\
According to the model, the surface of the sports field is a plane with equation $z=0$; the pipes are straight lines; one pipe, $P_{1}$, passes through the points $A(4,3,-3)$ and $B(-5,-7,-4)$; a different pipe, $P_{2}$, has equation $\frac{x-2}{3}=\frac{y-1}{4}=\frac{z+3}{-1}$; and the units are metres. \\
(a) Determine a vector equation of the line representing the pipe $P_{1}$. (2) \\
(b) Determine the coordinates of the point at which the pipe $P_{1}$ meets the surface of the playing field, according to the model. (2) \\
Determine, according to the model, \\
(c) the acute angle between pipes $P_{1}$ and $P_{2}$, giving your answer in degrees to 3 significant figures, (3) \\
(d) the shortest distance between pipes $P_{1}$ and $P_{2}$. (5)
@scheme
No mark scheme for this question. See the Model tab (F3) for the full worked answer.
@model
(a) Direction of $P_{1}$ is $\text{AB}=(-5-4,-7-3,-4-(-3))$. \\
$=(-9,-10,-1)$. \\
Thus $P_{1}$ is $\mathbf{r}=(4,3,-3)+t(-9,-10,-1)$. \\
(b) At the surface $z=0$. \\
$-3-t=0$ so $t=-3$. \\
Substitute into the line: \\
$(x,y,z)=(4,3,-3)-3(-9,-10,-1)$ \\
$=(31,33,0)$. \\
(c) The direction of $P_{2}$ is $\mathbf{v}=(3,4,-1)$. \\
For $P_{1}$, take $\mathbf{u}=(-9,-10,-1)$. \\
Use the scalar product: $\mathbf{u}\cdot\mathbf{v}=(-9)(3)+(-10)(4)+(-1)(-1)=-66$. \\
$|\mathbf{u}|=\sqrt{81+100+1}=\sqrt{182}$. \\
$|\mathbf{v}|=\sqrt{9+16+1}=\sqrt{26}$. \\
For the acute angle, $\cos\theta=\frac{66}{\sqrt{182}\sqrt{26}}$. \\
$\theta=16.372723...$ degrees. \\
$\theta=16.4$ degrees (3 sf). \\
(d) A point on $P_{2}$ is $Q=(2,1,-3)$. \\
Use the skew-line distance formula $d=\frac{|\text{AQ}\cdot(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v})|}{|\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}|}$. \\
$\text{AQ}=Q-A=(-2,-2,0)$. \\
$\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}=(14,-12,-6)$. \\
Check the components: $10+4=14$, $-9-3=-12$, $-36+30=-6$. \\
$\text{AQ}\cdot(\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v})=(-2)(14)+(-2)(-12)=-4$. \\
$|\mathbf{u}\times\mathbf{v}|=\sqrt{14^{2}+(-12)^{2}+(-6)^{2}}=2\sqrt{94}$. \\
$d=\frac{4}{2\sqrt{94}}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{94}}$. \\
$d=0.206284...$ m.

@q 7
@topic Two induction proofs
@marks 10
@question
(i) Prove by induction that, for all positive integers $n$, $\sum_{r=1}^{n}\frac{2}{(r+1)(r+2)}=\frac{n}{n+2}$. (5) \\
(ii) Prove by induction that, for all positive integers $n$, $f(n)=3^{2n+1}+2^{n+2}$ is divisible by 7. (5)
@scheme
No mark scheme for this question. See the Model tab (F3) for the full worked answer.
@model
(i) Base case $n=1$: left side $=\frac{2}{2\cdot3}=\frac{1}{3}$, and right side $=\frac{1}{1+2}=\frac{1}{3}$. \\
Assume $\sum_{r=1}^{k}\frac{2}{(r+1)(r+2)}=\frac{k}{k+2}$. \\
Add the term for $r=k+1$: \\
$\sum_{r=1}^{k+1}\frac{2}{(r+1)(r+2)}=\frac{k}{k+2}+\frac{2}{(k+2)(k+3)}$. \\
Use a common denominator: \\
$=\frac{k(k+3)+2}{(k+2)(k+3)}$. \\
$=\frac{k^{2}+3k+2}{(k+2)(k+3)}$. \\
$=\frac{(k+1)(k+2)}{(k+2)(k+3)}$. \\
$=\frac{k+1}{k+3}$, the formula for $n=k+1$. \\
Hence the formula holds for all positive integers. \\
(ii) Base case $n=1$: $f(1)=3^{3}+2^{3}=27+8=35=7(5)$. \\
Assume $f(k)=3^{2k+1}+2^{k+2}$ is divisible by 7. \\
Calculate the next value: \\
$f(k+1)=3^{2k+3}+2^{k+3}$. \\
$=9\cdot3^{2k+1}+2\cdot2^{k+2}$. \\
Rewrite 9 as $2+7$: \\
$=2(3^{2k+1}+2^{k+2})+7\cdot3^{2k+1}$. \\
$=2f(k)+7\cdot3^{2k+1}$. \\
Both terms are divisible by 7, so $f(k+1)$ is divisible by 7. \\
By induction, $f(n)$ is divisible by 7 for all positive integers.

@q 8
@topic Volume of revolution model
@marks 11
@question
Figure 1 shows the central vertical cross-section, $OABCDEO$, of the design for a solid glass ornament. Figure 2 shows the finite region $R$, which is bounded by the $y$-axis, the horizontal line $CB$, the vertical line $BA$, and the curve $AO$. The ornament is formed by rotating the region $R$ through 360 degrees about the $y$-axis. \\
The curve $AO$ is modelled by the equation $x=ky^{2}+3\sqrt{y}$, $0\leq y\leq4$, where $k$ is a constant. \\
The point $A$ has coordinates $(2.8,4)$ and the point $B$ has coordinates $(2.8,4.5)$. The units are centimetres. \\
(Diagram: From $y=0$ to $y=4$, the radius is the $x$ value of the curve. From $y=4$ to $y=4.5$, the radius is 2.8 cm.) \\
(a) Determine the value of $k$ according to this model. (2) \\
(b) Use algebraic integration to determine the exact volume of glass that would be required to make the ornament, according to the model. (7) \\
(c) State a limitation of the model. (1) \\
When the ornament was manufactured, 126 cubic centimetres of glass was required. \\
(d) Use this information and your answer to part (b) to evaluate the model, explaining your reasoning. (1)
@scheme
No mark scheme for this question. See the Model tab (F3) for the full worked answer.
@model
(a) At $A$, $y=4$ and $x=2.8$. \\
$2.8=k(4)^{2}+3\sqrt{4}$. \\
$2.8=16k+6$. \\
$16k=-3.2$. \\
$k=-\frac{1}{5}$. \\
(b) The curved part has radius $x=3\sqrt{y}-\frac{y^{2}}{5}$. \\
Use the disc method $V=\pi\int x^{2}\,dy$. \\
$x^{2}=(3\sqrt{y}-\frac{y^{2}}{5})^{2}$. \\
Expand: $x^{2}=9y-\frac{6}{5}y^{5/2}+\frac{1}{25}y^{4}$. \\
Integrate from $y=0$ to $y=4$: \\
$V_{\mathrm{curved}}=\pi[\frac{9}{2}y^{2}-\frac{12}{35}y^{7/2}+\frac{1}{125}y^{5}]_{0}^{4}$. \\
At 4, $4^{7/2}=128$ and $4^{5}=1024$. \\
$V_{\mathrm{curved}}=\pi(72-\frac{1536}{35}+\frac{1024}{125})$. \\
The neck is a cylinder with radius 2.8 and height $4.5-4=0.5$. \\
$V_{\mathrm{neck}}=\pi(2.8)^{2}(0.5)=\frac{98\pi}{25}$. \\
Add the two volumes: \\
$V=\pi(72-\frac{1536}{35}+\frac{1024}{125}+\frac{98}{25})$. \\
$=\frac{35198\pi}{875}$ cubic centimetres. \\
$\approx126.375$ cubic centimetres. \\
(c) The actual glass shape may not exactly follow the modelled curve. \\
(d) The model predicts about 126.375 cubic centimetres, close to the actual 126; it is a good estimate, slightly high by about 0.375 cubic centimetres.
